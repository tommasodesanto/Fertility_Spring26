## What the saved results already answer

A unit of the distribution is a household decision unit. The count of households with dependent children is the mass with dependent-count state greater than zero, evaluated after current fertility and housing choices. It is different from all households that have ever had children: empty nesters have positive parity and zero current dependent count.

## Actual state and timing

The state is wealth × tenure/product × location × adult age × income × parity × number of dependent children. Parity is 0, 1, 2, 3+; dependent count is 0, 1, 2, 3 and cannot exceed the parity index. A successful birth increments both parity and dependent count by one. At most one explicit successful birth occurs per household per four-year period; there is no twins/multiple-birth outcome or second explicit birth during the same period. The top parity has no further birth attempt. Dependency is an independent, memoryless departure process. Expected time at home is 18 years, implemented as 4.5 four-year periods. Each child independently leaves with probability 1/4.5 = 2/9 every transition. Thus next dependent count, conditional on a surviving household and current count m, is Binomial(m, 7/9). This is a count state, not a child's age or a shared clock reset by another birth. A child can leave by the first four-year transition, while about 28.462802% remain after five transitions (twenty years). No biological interpretation of such early departures is supplied by the state space. This is an explicit approximation, not evidence that a deterministic age-18 rule is implemented incorrectly. ## Top coding and the separate birth queue

An explicit third birth puts a household in the 3+ state, which is assigned a completed-fertility weight of 3.602359422009. The adjusted aggregate birth flow is explicit births plus 0.602359422009 times the flow entering top parity. These additional measured children enter aggregate renewal; they do not add dependent-count states, preferences, room requirements or separate birth dates. Consequently, a weighted count of children ever born and the sum of current dependent counts are deliberately different objects. Do not apply the top-code weight indiscriminately to each current m=3 cell and call it a consistent person count. ## Adults, couples, and resident persons

The flow-utility equivalence scale is normalized to a childless couple, using ((2 + 0.7m)/2)^0.7 before the CRRA adjustment. This does not imply that every unit contains two observed adults: the economic state has no marriage status, spouse age, spouse survival, nonhead adult count, group-quarters residence or union-formation/dissolution process. Household-head empirical mass anchors and a couple-normalized utility function cannot be converted into a resident total simply by multiplying by two. Source: [code/model/intergen_eqscale_seq_optimized/solver.py:2238](/Users/tommasodesanto/Desktop/Projects/Fertility/Fertility_Spring26/tmp/e5f_fertility_nest_compute_20260907a/code/model/intergen_eqscale_seq_optimized/solver.py:2238).

Therefore H + sum(mg), 2H + sum(mg), or H plus the queue are not verified resident-person totals. The queue contains future household entrants, not a current child stock. Raw m counts do support the internal dependent-unit stock and its within-model distribution; they do not identify all biological children, ages under 18, spouses, adult children at home, or all residents.

## Worked identities and classification

With 100 surviving households, each with two dependent units, and no new births or household exits, one maturation step leaves 200 × 7/9 = 155.556 dependent units on average. The number of households with at least one dependent is 100 × (1 − (2/9)^2) = 95.062. Household count remains 100; the 44.444 departed dependent units do not directly create new households. Separately, an adjusted birth flow of 21 schedules 21/2.1 = 10 entrant households twenty years later. If a later transition starts with 100 households, loses 3 and receives 5 scheduled entrants, its next count is exactly 102.

No new implementation bug was demonstrated. The count-state duration, top coding and entry queue are maintained approximations. The missing resident/headship identity is an unresolved closure if these outputs are presented as a population forecast. The stale person-package integration sentence is a documentation discrepancy. The immediate household-with-children comparison is measurable already; a resident-person forecast needs the separately specified person law.

## Additional timing and parental-exit verification

Newborns receive no separate first-period protection. The fertility operator inserts the successful-birth mass into m+1 in g_post_fertility; the next-period operator applies Pi_child to all those child-count cells. Therefore 22.222% leave by the first four-year advance, 63.405% by sixteen years, and 28.463% remain after twenty years. See `fertility_nested.py:130`, `run_e5f_open_population_transition.py:850`, and `solver.py:5403` in the hashed frozen worktree. These are duration implications of the specified memoryless count process, not measured child-age hazards.

When the parental household exits, all its child-state mass leaves the household distribution. The operator computes survivors as survival times the full post-fertility cohort, drops the oldest cohort, and has no orphan or reassignment ledger. Its diagnostic matured-child difference compares surviving households before and after advancement and therefore does not record children removed with parental exits as a distinct person flow. A maintained person ledger cannot be created by adding these dependent counts to separately propagated person cohorts: that would double-count overlap and omit unresolved transfers.

The exact empirical parent-versus-nonparent rooms/ownership sample definition (under-18 linked child versus NCHILD/all linked children) was not verified in this bounded pass. The current code-state definition is established; empirical equivalence is not. No sample definition or target was changed.
