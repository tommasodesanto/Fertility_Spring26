\[
z_J=(s_J,\Gamma_\lambda(s_J))

must lie on the local stable graph’s feasible neighborhood; the reform must be permanent so the same autonomous \(g_\lambda\) applies thereafter; and the graph must be locally forward invariant. Then \(g_\lambda^m(z_J)\to z^*(\lambda)\). A terminally closed numerical path does not verify any of these interval-wide conditions.

Suggested compact replacement for the finite-state claim:

> With fixed entrant distribution, two-period lives, and unique measurable household policies, every old cohort generated after date \(t\) is \(G_{t+1}=K(\eta_t)\), where \(\eta_t\) contains every current and anticipated object entering that cohort’s two-period problem. Thus the equilibrium can be finite-dimensional on this model-generated family of distributions. It is not finite-dimensional for arbitrary initial \(G\). After an unexpected reform, retain the inherited \(G_0=K(\eta_{-1}^{\rm pre})\); cohorts choosing after the announcement use the policy and prices then anticipated.
