# Independent review: heterogeneity and the stationary population condition

September 5, 2026. Scope: proof sections 8–9. The requested measure normalization and uniform operator/initial-old conditions have been made explicit in section 9.

## Judgments

1. **Section 9 exact aggregation: valid, conditional on stated uniform branches.** At \((\epsilon,\chi,\tau^p)=(0,0,0)\), with \(d_i=b_i/(1-\phi)\) and \(w_i=y_i+b_i\),
\[
h_i=d_i/P,\quad n_i=h_i/2,\quad
x_i=\frac{w_i-d_i+qd_iP_{t+1}/P_t}{\rho}.
\]
Saving, post-mortgage financial assets, old consumption, old housing, and estate are linear in \((w_i,d_i)\) on the owner branch. Hence their integrals use only \(E[w_i]=1.15\) and \(E[d_i]=1\), regardless of \(y,b\) correlation. The aggregate recurrence and the actual initial-old housing demand are therefore exactly (T1)–(T2), provided the old cohort is the one generated by the pre-reform type-specific owner choices.

The purchase-wedge condition is correctly
\[
\frac{w_i}{d_i}>\frac{107}{100}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
y_i>\frac{87}{20}b_i.
\]
Its worst corner is correctly
\[
.94-\frac{87}{20}(.205)=.04825>0.
\]
I independently checked the other owner margins at the limiting stationary prices. With \(\rho=58/25\),
\[
x_i=y_i/\rho,\quad a'_i=y_i(1-1/\rho),\quad
h_i^2=\frac{3y_i}{4\rho},\quad e_i=\frac{5y_i}{4\rho}.
\]
Their worst rectangle margins include saving \(>.534\), retention \(>.664\), estate slack \(>.202\), and owner-cap slack \(\ge .975\). Thus corner verification is legitimate because these expressions are affine.

Conditional renters are also uniformly regular at the limit:
\[
x_i^R=\frac{w_i-.09}{2.2},\qquad h_i^R=.25,\qquad n_i^R=.125.
\]
Over the rectangle, \(x_i^R\ge .475\), saving exceeds \(.60\), the young rental-cap wedge exceeds \(.81\), and the old rental-cap wedge exceeds \(.085\). Thus both renter caps remain strictly binding in a common neighborhood.

Precise wording correction: call these **housing-cap wedges/multipliers**, rather than “housing-value gaps,” unless the latter is explicitly defined as the marginal value minus user cost.

2. **Section 9 smooth heterogeneous extension: substantively valid but incomplete unless its measure and operator conditions are stated.** Fixed compact support plus uniform strict conditional branches gives uniformly bounded first and second derivatives. Differentiation under a fixed probability distribution \(F\) is then valid, including endogenous tenure sorting through the logistic \(\pi^O(i)\). The date-zero demand must integrate over the *actual* pre-reform state:
\[
G_0=K_{\mu}^{\rm pre}(F),
\]
including type-specific assets, titles, and tenure probabilities. Section 9 correctly does this rather than replacing old households by a mean state.

It is also correct not to claim that a finite vector of cross-sectional moments summarizes heterogeneous old states once \(\epsilon>0\). A finite list of dated price arguments may parameterize the model-generated distribution in this two-period model, but the theorem needs only smoothness of the integrated old-demand map.

Missing conditions to add:

- \(F\) is a probability measure and a young cohort’s type measure is \(Y_tF\); otherwise the mean-based aggregation and housing normalization are ambiguous.
- The initial old cohort is exactly the pre-reform stationary distribution generated by \(F\), including tenure selection.
- Logistic tastes are independent of \((y,b)\), as specified; otherwise the stated separable integration formula must be replaced by the relevant joint distribution.
- All relevant conditional branches—including old retention/estate branches—are uniformly strict on one compact type/price/transfer neighborhood.
- The integrated operator is \(C^1\) as a map on the weighted sequence space, with all required price leads (through \(P_{t+2}\) and corresponding transfers) included and a uniformly small bounded derivative perturbation.

With those additions, this does remove homogeneous entrants from the headline theorem for this bounded-support class. It does not cover broad distributions crossing constraints, nor does it yield a finite-dimensional heterogeneous state summary.

Minimal theorem wording:

> Let \(F\) be a fixed probability distribution on the stated rectangle with the stated marginal means, and let ownership tastes be independent logistic draws. If all conditional owner and renter branches are uniformly strict on a compact neighborhood of the all-owner limiting equilibrium, and the inherited old cohort is the actual pre-reform distribution \(K_\mu^{\rm pre}(F)\), then for sufficiently small positive \((\epsilon,\chi,\tau^p)\) and a sufficiently small credit reform, a locally unique exponentially convergent equilibrium exists. Initial fertility and terminal household population rise. At the limiting economy, its aggregate transition is exactly the homogeneous transition.

3. **Section 8: valid.** At replacement, \(n^*=1/\nu\), and the original stationary owner budget gives
\[
X=\frac{y+b-\chi/\nu-(1-q)d}{\rho}.
\]
The fertility FOC yields the stated \(h^*\); the old FOCs yield
\[
h^{2*}=\frac{\beta\gamma}{q(1-q)}\frac{Xh^*}{d},
\]
so (T8) follows directly. Differentiating \(\log Y^*=-\log[h^*(1+gX/d)]+\text{constant}\) gives exactly (T9). The signs reported are consistent: \(+.2565939892\) for \(\chi=.015,\gamma=.3\), and negative for \(\gamma=.001,\chi=.015\).

One terminology correction: \((\alpha+\vartheta)X>(1-q)d+\chi/\nu\) is the strict **purchase-cap wedge** condition; the down-payment constraint itself binds at \(h=d/P\). Positive saving and the other original inequalities remain separate assumptions. This is stationary all-owner comparative statics only; it establishes neither a general transition nor a Pareto result.
