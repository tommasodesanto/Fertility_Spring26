# Direct utilitarian housing comparison

Independent analytical review, September 8, 2026. This note concerns the
conventional-finance model in
[the discussion draft](/Users/tommasodesanto/Desktop/Projects/Fertility/Fertility_Spring26/latex/JMP_DS_suggestions/simplified_olg_conventional_finance.tex).
It makes no numerical, calibration, or transition-existence claim. Only this
review file was written.

**Main result.** Under equal weights on each living household's remaining
utility, the primitive restriction \(\boxed{\beta\ge q}\), together with the
existing conditions that guarantee a positive mass of strictly constrained
owners with slack housing caps and old estate floors, suffices for a local
utilitarian reallocation of housing from current old owners to current young
owners. Fertility, tenure, consumption, estates, every future real allocation,
and the net external asset path can all remain fixed. This is an uncompensated
utilitarian gain: the donating old households lose utility. It is distinct
from a Pareto improvement and from a complete first-best characterization.

## 1. Welfare normalization and the short proposition

Use the model's original cardinal utility normalization: the coefficient on
young adult consumption \(\log x\) and on old consumption \(\log c^2\) is one.
Young lifetime utility is \(U_i=u_i^y+\beta V_{i,t+1}\). Let the current social
criterion, up to terms unaffected by the intervention, be
\[
\mathcal W_t^{\rho}
=\int U_i\,dM_t^Y(i)+\rho\int u_j^o\,dM_t^O(j),\qquad \rho>0.
\tag{1}
\]
The measures count actual households, rather than normalizing each age group's
total welfare to an average. Equal weights on remaining utility mean
\(\rho=1\). Equal weights on the original lifetime utilities of successive
birth cohorts mean \(\rho=\beta\): the old period already carried that factor
when each current old household was young. These are different welfare
criteria. Multiplying an individual's utility by a different positive
constant changes a utilitarian comparison unless its welfare weight is
adjusted inversely.

**Proposition.** Consider a positive stationary equilibrium. Suppose a
positive measure of young owners has a strictly positive mortgage multiplier,
slack young housing caps, and slack caps and estate floors when old. Pair
these owners with the current old owners generated by the same stationary
primitive types and owner choices. If
\[
\boxed{\beta/q\ge\rho,}
\tag{2}
\]
and \(L=(1-\phi+q\tau^p)P>0\), a sufficiently small direct reallocation
increases current aggregate young housing and \(\mathcal W_t^\rho\). It keeps
fertility, tenure, all current consumption, all estates, every future real
allocation, existing creditors' payments, and net external assets unchanged.
The planner may waive the young household's individual borrowing restriction.

For remaining-utility weights, (2) is \(\beta\ge q\). For birth-normalized
lifetime weights, it holds strictly for every \(\beta>0\), because the model
assumes \(0<q<1\). The draft's \(\phi\in(0,1)\) and nonnegative property
tax ensure \(L>0\).

## 2. Proof from the original budgets

At any date define
\[
A_t=(1+q\tau^p)P_t,\quad p_t=A_t-qP_{t+1}>0,
\quad L_t=A_t-\phi_tP_t>0.
\]
Let \(\Lambda_i=1/x_i\) be the young household's current-income marginal
utility and \(\mu_i\ge0\) multiply its restriction
\(qa_i'+\phi_tP_th_i\ge0\). Because old owners can resize, their value
depends on inherited assets and title only through
\(a_i'+P_{t+1}h_i+y_i^o+T_{t+1}\). Its marginal value is
\(m_{i,t+1}=1/c_{i,t+1}^2\). The saving and housing first-order conditions
give, when the young housing cap is slack,
\[
\Lambda_i=\frac{\beta}{q}m_{i,t+1}+\mu_i,
\qquad
\frac{\alpha}{s_i}
=\frac{\beta}{q}p_t m_{i,t+1}+\mu_iL_t.
\tag{3}
\]
An old owner with slack housing cap and estate floor has
\[
\frac{\gamma}{h_j^2}=p_t m_{j,t},\qquad m_{j,t}=1/c_j^2.
\tag{4}
\]
For a matched stationary predecessor, \(m_{j,t}=m_{i,t+1}=m_i\): entering
types, conditional owner policies, prices, and rebates repeat. Consequently,
\[
\frac{\alpha}{s_i}-\rho\frac{\gamma}{h_i^2}
=\left(\frac{\beta}{q}-\rho\right)pm_i+\mu_iL>0.
\tag{5}
\]
This compares raw cardinal marginal utilities, as required by the explicitly
chosen utilitarian criterion. It does not use consumption-compensated housing
values or the draft's separate condition \(\beta K L\ge p-L\).

For completeness, the improving direction has an exact financial settlement.
Give the young owner \(\epsilon\) additional housing and reduce the matched
old owner's current housing by \(\epsilon\). Keep their consumption and
estates fixed. Make the financial adjustments
\[
\Delta a_i'=-P_{t+1}\epsilon,
\qquad \Delta a_j^e=qP_{t+1}\epsilon,
\tag{6}
\]
and transfer \(p_t\epsilon\) from the old household to the young household.
The young budget's extra cost is
\(q\Delta a_i'+A_t\epsilon=p_t\epsilon\). The old cash budget frees exactly
\(A_t\epsilon-\Delta a_j^e=p_t\epsilon\). Its estate is unchanged because
\(q^{-1}\Delta a_j^e-P_{t+1}\epsilon=0\), and the young household's next-period
resources are unchanged because
\(\Delta a_i'+P_{t+1}\epsilon=0\). The added old financial saving exactly
funds the added young debt; there is no net external borrowing. Total owner
housing, property-tax revenue, and existing claims are unchanged. The old
saver's nonnegativity restriction is relaxed by its larger saving. The young
mortgage restriction may be violated, which is precisely the restriction the
direct planner is allowed to waive.

All future real choices can therefore remain fixed. The exact welfare change
per matched pair is
\[
\Delta\mathcal W^\rho(\epsilon)
=\alpha\log(1+\epsilon/s_i)
 +\rho\gamma\log(1-\epsilon/h_i^2).
\tag{7}
\]
Its derivative at zero is (5), so a sufficiently small positive transfer is
strictly improving and preserves positivity and the young cap. This is not
compensation: the old household pays the transfer while keeping its goods
consumption and estate and losing housing services.

In a continuum, the matching uses the common stationary owner measure
\(N\pi_O(i)\,dF(i)\), not an assumed equal mass of arbitrarily selected income
groups. A positive-measure eligible set can be restricted to a positive-measure
subset with uniform positive value gap, housing slack, and positive log
arguments, using a countable union of sets with margins bounded below by
\(1/k\). Equation (7) then permits a common small transfer on that subset.
One exceptional type of zero mass would not suffice.

## 3. Existing primitive conditions supply the required owner mass

No new existence construction is needed. Retain conditions **(E), (I), and
(C)** of
[the stationary primitives review](/Users/tommasodesanto/Desktop/Projects/Fertility/Fertility_Spring26/output/model/simplified_olg_amendments/conventional_stationary_primitives_review.md),
with \(d_L>0\). They are the draft's low-price replacement-fertility
inequalities, positive-mass future-income inequality, and estate/cap
inequalities. In particular, on a positive-\(F\)-mass set \(S\), they require
\[
qv_{0i}>kw_{0i}+(k-q)_+\bar T,
\quad
\omega_Bd_p>q\gamma,
\quad
h_O^{\max}>
\frac{M_S}{d_pP_-}\max\left\{1,\frac{\gamma}{qK}\right\}.
\tag{8}
\]
All quantities in (8) are primitive bounds defined in that existing review:
\(d_p=1-q+q\tau^p\), \(K=1+\gamma+\omega_B\),
\(k=D/C_{\min}-1\), and \(P_-,\bar T,M_S\) are its explicit price,
rebate, and lifetime-resource bounds.

The reviewed inequalities ensure stationary existence and, at every stationary
equilibrium, strict finance and slack young and old caps and estate floors on
\(S\). Finite logistic tenure values give
\(\int_S\pi_O(i)dF(i)>0\). The stationary predecessor distribution supplies
the matched current old owners automatically. Add only \(\beta\ge q\) for
remaining-utility weights; the separate lifetime-housing condition **(H)**
is unnecessary for the present utilitarian result. The earlier analytic
compatibility construction already permits arbitrary positive \(\beta\),
so this extra restriction does not require a new family. These are sufficient
theoretical restrictions, not evidence that a calibration meets them.

## 4. The direct housing optimum and the aggregate limitation

A general conditional housing optimum is explicit once welfare weights,
fertility, tenure, and future estates are fixed. Let \(a_i>0\) and \(b_j>0\)
be the weights on current young and old felicity. Put
\[
C_n=\kappa\int n_i\,dM^Y(i),\qquad
\bar s_i=h_{m_i}^{\max}-\kappa n_i,
\]
and let \(\bar h_j=h_{m_j}^{\max}\) for old renters and
\(\bar h_j=\min\{h_O^{\max},e_j/P_{t+1}\}\) for old owners. The latter
retains the original estate floor when the estate is held fixed. Maximizing
the original utilities over current housing, subject to the fixed stock,
has the unique housing allocation
\[
s_i^*=\min\{\bar s_i,a_i\alpha/\lambda_H\},\qquad
h_j^{2*}=\min\{\bar h_j,b_j\gamma/\lambda_H\},
\tag{9}
\]
where \(\lambda_H>0\) clears
\(\int s_i^*dM^Y+\int h_j^{2*}dM^O=\bar H-C_n\). This assumes some aggregate
capacity slack; if the entire feasible stock exactly fills all caps, every
quantity is already fixed by its cap. Strict concavity supplies uniqueness.

If current goods are also freely redistributed with their total use fixed,
their problem separates: \(x_i^*=a_i/\lambda_C\) and
\(c_j^{2*}=b_j/\lambda_C\), with \(\lambda_C\) clearing the available adult
goods. All \(\omega_B\log e_j\) terms remain in welfare but are constants
in this fixed-estate comparison. If estates are instead chosen jointly while
their floor is retained, the housing and estate conditions are coupled by the
floor multiplier; that is a different program.

When every cap in (9) is slack, \(a_i=1\), and \(b_j=\rho\), aggregate young
housing is
\[
H_Y^*=C_n+
\frac{\alpha Y}{\alpha Y+\rho\gamma O}(\bar H-C_n).
\tag{10}
\]
With heterogeneous fertility or binding caps, use (9) instead. The existence
of a strictly improving old-to-young pair does **not** establish that the
complete optimum in (9) has more aggregate young housing than the competitive
allocation. Redistribution within each age group and other binding caps can
change the aggregate comparison. Nor is (9) the full dynamic first best:
future allocations and fertility have deliberately been fixed.

## 5. What is financing-specific, and what holds along a path

Without a financing wedge, the matched stationary gap is
\((\beta/q-\rho)pm_i\). It can be positive solely because of the chosen
intertemporal utility weights. In particular, with remaining-utility weights,
\(\boxed{\beta=q}\) removes this ordinary matched-type intertemporal term;
the housing gap becomes exactly \(\mu_iL\). With birth-normalized lifetime
weights, the gap is
\(\beta(1/q-1)pm_i+\mu_iL\), so it remains positive even when finance is
unconstrained. General utilitarian redistribution across rich and poor types
can remain profitable even at \(\beta=q\); that equality isolates finance
only in the matched-type age comparison.

The corresponding fixed-price marginal welfare gain from a current monetary
transfer from an old household to a young household is
\(\Lambda_i-\rho m_j\). At a matched stationary pair it equals
\((\beta/q-\rho)m_i+\mu_i\). This observation is useful for the separate
constrained-transfer analysis, but neither it nor (7) proves that a policy
restricted to transfers and private reoptimization clears housing at unchanged
prices or retains the same sign after prices adjust.

At a transition date the exact direct-housing test is instead
\[
p_t\left(\frac{\beta}{q}m_{i,t+1}-\rho m_{j,t}\right)
 +\mu_iL_t>0,
\tag{11}
\]
for a young recipient below its cap and an old donor with slack cap and estate
floor. For remaining-utility weights,
\(\beta m_{i,t+1}/q\ge m_{j,t}\) is a sufficient dated condition when
\(\mu_i>0\). Stationarity supplied the equality of the two old-age marginal
utilities in (5); it cannot be imposed along an arbitrary adjustment path.
The existing stationary income and capacity bounds do not establish eligible
mass at every transition date.

This dated welfare test needs no convergence assumption: it applies to any
already existing path at a date where its hypotheses hold. It has no fertility
effect because fertility is held fixed. Fertility responses to a mortgage
policy, existence of the resulting equilibrium path, and convergence to a
positive stationary endpoint remain separate questions.

**Verification.** The first-order conditions were derived directly from the
draft's young mortgage budget and old resizing problem. Equations (6) verify
both original dated cash budgets, estate settlement, next-period net resources,
and aggregate financial cancellation algebraically. The stationary mass claim
uses only the existing reviewed primitive coverage conditions; no simulation,
new parameter family, or numerical neighborhood claim was used.

## 6. Follow-up: an aggregate corollary with an explicit regime

This section strengthens the local result to the entire **conditional current
housing optimum**. It keeps equal remaining-utility weights, fixed fertility
and tenure, and fixed future real allocations and estates. It does not use
birth-normalized lifetime weights to generate the age comparison.

Let \(Q\) be the probability measure over entering endowments and realized
tenure: \(dQ(i,m)=\pi_m(i)dF(i)\). Conditional housing and fertility do not
depend further on the ownership taste. At stationarity, both cohorts have
mass \(N\), and the current old distribution is the pushforward of this same
measure. Thus \(\mathbb E_Q n=1/\nu\), with no representative-household or
equal-income assumption.

**Aggregate corollary.** Suppose \(\beta\ge q\), every current old household
has slack housing cap and, for owners, slack estate floor, and the uncapped
conditional housing optimum respects all young caps, all old caps, and all
retained old estate floors. Then
\[
H_Y^*-H_Y^{\rm eq}
=\frac{N}{\alpha+\gamma}
 \mathbb E_Q[\alpha h^o-\gamma s]\ge0.
\tag{12}
\]
The inequality is strict when a positive measure of owners is strictly
mortgage-constrained and below its young cap. The existing eligible-owner
conditions supply this strictness. More generally, a positive young borrowing
or housing-cap multiplier on positive measure also gives strictness.

**Proof.** Write \(L_O=L\), \(L_R=p\), and normalize the renter's borrowing
multiplier to multiply \(qa'\ge0\). For either tenure, the stationary
first-order conditions, including a possible young housing-cap multiplier
\(\eta_i^y\ge0\), are
\[
\frac{\alpha}{s_i}
=\frac{\beta}{q}p m_i+\mu_iL_{m_i}+\eta_i^y
\ge pm_i=\frac{\gamma}{h_i^o}.
\tag{13}
\]
Therefore \(\gamma s_i\le\alpha h_i^o\) type by type, strictly on the
eligible constrained-owner set. Integration is legitimate under the common
stationary measure \(Q\). The uncapped optimum gives every young household
adult space
\[
s^*=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\gamma}
      \mathbb E_Q[s+h^o]
\]
and every old household housing
\(h^{o*}=\gamma\mathbb E_Q[s+h^o]/(\alpha+\gamma)\). Its young aggregate
includes the same fixed child space \(\kappa N/\nu\) as equilibrium.
Subtracting the equilibrium aggregate yields (12). Retained caps and floors
were assumed to permit this allocation, so strict concavity makes it the
conditional optimum, not just a candidate. Young equilibrium caps themselves
need not be slack for this proof; their multipliers strengthen (13).

At \(\boxed{\beta=q}\), the exact aggregate difference becomes
\[
\boxed{\quad
H_Y^*-H_Y^{\rm eq}
=\frac{N}{\alpha+\gamma}
 \mathbb E_Q[(\mu_iL_{m_i}+\eta_i^y)s_i h_i^o].
\quad}
\tag{14}
\]
If young equilibrium caps are also slack, only the financing multipliers
remain. Renter no-borrowing restrictions can contribute alongside owner
mortgage restrictions. A positive constrained-owner mass alone is enough for
strictness. This is an aggregate financing result under the stated regime;
at \(\beta>q\), the separate ordinary intertemporal term also contributes.

## 7. A noncircular primitive way to guarantee that regime

The draft's replacement-fertility condition gives a stronger price floor than
the earlier \(P_-\). Write \(\theta=\vartheta\),
\(d_L=1-\phi+q\tau^p\), \(d_{\max}=\max\{d_p,d_L\}\), and
\(D=1+\alpha+\theta+\beta K\). Define
\[
\delta=\theta\nu-\frac{\chi D}{\underline w}>0,
\qquad
P_0=\frac{\underline w\,\delta}{D d_{\max}\kappa}>0,
\qquad \underline p=d_pP_0.
\tag{15}
\]
Condition (E) implies
\(h_R^{\max}>\kappa/\nu+\alpha\kappa/\delta\).
At a stationary equilibrium there is a type-tenure choice with
\(n\le1/\nu\). Its original fertility first-order condition and the reviewed
bound \(x\ge\underline w/D\) give
\[
\frac{\alpha\kappa}{s}
=\frac{\theta}{n}-\frac{\chi}{x}\ge\delta,
\qquad
h=s+\kappa n\le
\frac{\alpha\kappa}{\delta}+\frac{\kappa}{\nu}<h_R^{\max}.
\]
That choice's young cap is therefore slack. Its housing first-order condition
implies \(\alpha x/s\le d_{\max}P\), and consequently
\(\boxed{P\ge P_0}\). Unlike \(P_-\), this floor is independent of both
housing caps. No aggregate fertility monotonicity or transition argument is
being assumed.

Let
\[
M_*\ge\mathop{\rm ess\,sup}_{i\in F}
  \{w_{0i}+qv_{0i}+(1+q)\bar T\}<\infty.
\]
Together with (E) and the original bounded-endowment assumptions, the
following conservative **global** conditions suffice:
\[
\boxed{\quad
h_R^{\max}>\frac{(1+q^{-1})M_*}{\underline p},
\qquad h_O^{\max}>h_R^{\max},
\quad}
\tag{16}
\]
\[
\boxed{\quad
\omega_Bd_p>q\gamma,
\qquad
\omega_B\beta(\alpha+\gamma)d_p\underline w
  >\gamma qD\bar M.
\quad}
\tag{17}
\]
Here \(\bar M\) is the existing upper bound on mean lifetime resources.
The first part of (17) is the original old-owner floor condition; its second
part guarantees floor slack also at the planner's allocation when each old
estate is retained. Neither \(\alpha\ge\gamma\) nor equilibrium floor slack
alone supplies that second guarantee.

To verify (16), the original lifetime budget bounds equilibrium young housing
by \(M_*/p\) and old housing by \(M_*/(qp)\). It also implies
\[
\mathbb E_Q[s+h^o]<\frac{\bar M}{qp}
\le\frac{M_*}{qp},\qquad
\kappa n_i<\frac{M_*}{p}.
\]
The uncapped planner's old housing is below \(M_*/(qp)\), and each young
household's total housing is below
\((1+q^{-1})M_*/p\). Since \(p\ge\underline p\), (16) makes every physical
cap slack both in equilibrium and at the conditional planner optimum. It
also makes the old unconstrained housing formula feasible for all types.
These are finite inequalities whose right-hand sides do not depend on either
cap; enlarging the renter cap no longer weakens the price bound.

For (17), old equilibrium choices are
\(h^o=\gamma z/(Kp)\) and \(e=\omega_Bz/(Kq)\). The young budget and saving
conditions imply
\[
\lambda=\frac{\beta K}{qz}\le\frac1x,
\qquad
e\ge\frac{\omega_B\beta x}{q^2}
\ge\frac{\omega_B\beta\underline w}{q^2D}.
\]
The planner's common old housing satisfies
\[
Ph^{o*}
<\frac{\gamma\bar M}{(\alpha+\gamma)q d_p}.
\]
The second inequality in (17) makes this last quantity smaller than every
owner's retained estate. Hence the effective cap \(e_i/P\) is slack as well.

Adding the existing positive-mass income condition (I) ensures strict finance
on its set \(S\). The global bounds above already provide the cap and floor
coverage required in its proof, including for the finance-relaxed comparison.
Together with \(\beta\ge q\), they therefore give stationary existence and
the strict aggregate corollary in primitives. Their purpose is analytical
coverage; they are more conservative than the exact regime test and are not
asserted to fit empirical housing caps. No new parameter family is needed or
claimed here.

## 8. Remaining active-cap boundary

The aggregate result in (12) is established when the uncapped planner allocation
is feasible. With active planner caps, equation (9), rather than (10), determines
the optimum, and (12) is no longer its aggregate difference. Positive
pointwise gaps cannot simply be integrated and inserted into the uncapped
formula in that case.

The original fertility first-order condition does restrict child space:
\[
\kappa n_i<\frac{\theta}{\alpha}s_i,
\qquad
\frac{s_i}{h_i}>\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\theta}.
\tag{18}
\]
It rules out arbitrary fertility-space combinations and was useful in deriving
(15). It does not itself make each residual planner capacity
\(h_{m_i}^{\max}-\kappa n_i\) exceed the common desired adult space.
Similarly, \(\alpha\ge\gamma\) orders the uncapped young and old quantities
but does not check these residual capacities or the heterogeneous retained
estate caps. This follow-up therefore makes no general aggregate claim for
arbitrary active planner caps. It provides a proved regime with explicit
primitive coverage, not a counterexample to every possible broader theorem.
