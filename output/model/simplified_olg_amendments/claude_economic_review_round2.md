## (i) Corrected verdict

I accept objections 1, 2, 5 and 6 in full and 3 and 4 in substance. φ is the purchase LTV and φ/q is the balloon relative to unchanged house value; the "not an LTV" remark is withdrawn. The formula x_i = w_i/(E+βKe_i) needs slack young caps, and qEv_i > βKw_i covers only uncapped households; both are withdrawn, and the proposition below needs neither. The retention repair is withdrawn. The slide line "income gap plus equity term > 0 iff φ<q" is withdrawn; the total can be negative.

The real error, in the note and in my review, is reading the tilt through resources. Read it through the Euler inequality. With the old unconstrained (E1 below), every young household, either tenure, capped or not, constrained or not, satisfies

c^o_i / x_i ≥ β/q,

with equality exactly when its finance constraint is slack. Every young household satisfies s_i ≤ αx_i/p, since the housing first-order condition is α/s_i = p/x_i + μ_i qθ + η_i with both multipliers nonnegative and θ = (1−φ/q)P ≥ 0. Every old household has h^o_i = γc^o_i/p. Therefore

αh^o_i − γs_i = (αγ/p)(c^o_i − x_i) + γ(αx_i/p − s_i),

second term nonnegative. So if βR_f ≥ 1, then c^o_i ≥ x_i for all i, and both planner tests in the note, c̄^o ≥ x̄ and αh̄^o ≥ γs̄, hold. No income comparison, no all-constrained benchmark, no φ = q. The boxed condition v̄/K > w̄/E is what βR_f ≥ 1 looks like after the φ = q, all-constrained specialization, where x_i = w_i/E and c^o_i = v_i/K. It read as an income assumption because the specialization hid the Euler content.

The decision this forces: in the frozen model, the direction of the equal-weight dated planner result is a statement about patience against the world interest rate. Housing finance, children and equity determine the size of the misallocation, which households gain, the young space wedge, and tenure sorting. They do not determine the sign. If β < q, an unconstrained household has c^o_i < x_i and the planner moves goods toward the old for it; no primitive theorem then exists on this criterion. If the author wants the sign itself to come from finance, the criterion has to change from c̄^o versus x̄ to the Euler wedge μ_i > 0, a constrained young household would borrow at R_f. That is a different, Pareto-flavored statement and is not in the note. The current note has not earned the preferred equilibrium theorem in the form "finance and children imply misallocation to the old"; it has earned "given βR_f ≥ 1, the planner moves goods and housing to the young, strictly when any young constraint binds."

On old larger homes (objection 4): agreed, and it is worse than I said. Under E1 an old owner spends the share γ/K of its resources on housing, so for a constrained owner h^o_i ≥ h^y_i iff

γ v_i ≥ (Kp − γθ) h^y_i.

E1 itself gives ω_B ≥ qγ/(1−q+qτ), hence Kp ≥ p + γ(1+qτ)P, hence Kp − γθ ≥ p + γqτP > 0 for every q, φ ≤ q, τ ≥ 0. So with v_i = 0 the inequality fails identically. Equity alone can never deliver old larger homes once the estate floor is slack; old income must cover the balloon, old consumption and carrying cost, exactly as your budget shows. The cause is free resizing, which the frozen note chose, together with two-period timing, which makes a binding young constraint coincide with a lifetime resource tilt. Neither is fixable inside the note. The right response is to drop the word "retain" and the claim h̄^o > h̄^y from the theorem; the planner result does not use it.

## (ii) One recommended formulation

Setting: positive stationary equilibrium, τ ≥ 0 with rebate T, φ ∈ (0,q], p = (1−q+qτ)P, θ = (1−φ/q)P, all tenure choices, caps and financing constraints as in the environment.

(E1) Old unconstrained: ω_B(1−q+qτ) ≥ qγ and old choices below their size limits.
(E2) βR_f ≥ 1.

Proposition. Under E1 and E2, at the reference equilibrium c^o_i ≥ x_i for every i, and αh̄^o ≥ γs̄. Both hold strictly if β > q or if any young household has a binding finance constraint or size limit. The dated planner then moves goods to the young, and, when its size limits are slack or the note's capacity condition holds, moves housing to the young. The premises of the existing fertility Proposition 2 follow.

Proof sketch. E1 makes old value K log z_i and old housing γc^o_i/p. The young first-order conditions in a′ and h give the Euler inequality and the s_i bound above; the multiplier μ_i is zero exactly when finance is slack. E2 turns c^o_i/x_i ≥ β/q into c^o_i ≥ x_i. Sum the identity. Everything is proved; nothing is assumed about incomes, about who is constrained, or about old versus young home sizes.

Not proved, and needing work: (a) that the sign holds for β < q; (b) any statement that h̄^o > h̄^y, which requires γv̄ to cover (Kp − γθ)h̄^y and is an income condition; (c) magnitudes, which need the equilibrium μ_i and are where φ, κ, χ and the caps enter.

## (iii) Is keeping φ general and letting some young save the right next step?

Yes, and it is substantive, because the proposition above already does both and shows what they buy. Letting households save is what exposes that the sign rests on βR_f ≥ 1 and not on the all-constrained benchmark. Keeping φ < q is what makes the young space wedge μ_i qθ nonzero, so binding finance shows up in housing and not only in goods; at φ = q the wedge is identically zero and a mortgage limit is a no-borrowing constraint. This step also removes the E-versus-cap error in my E2, since μ_i ≥ 0 is all that is used. Do this before any friction or age structure. The one thing it will not do is make old homes larger; if the author needs that as a theorem rather than a description, the frozen model cannot supply it without an income condition, and the note should say so.

## (iv) Proposed slide

Stationary equilibrium, τ ≥ 0, φ ∈ (0,q], p = (1−q+qτ)P, θ = (1−φ/q)P.
Assume ω_B(1−q+qτ) ≥ qγ, old choices below caps, and βR_f ≥ 1.
Then for every young household c^o_i/x_i ≥ β/q, strict iff its mortgage limit binds, and s_i ≤ αx_i/p, strict iff a finance or size constraint binds.
For every old household h^o_i = γc^o_i/p.
Hence c̄^o ≥ x̄ and αh̄^o ≥ γs̄: the planner moves goods and housing to the young, strictly if any young constraint binds.
The sign comes from patience against R_f; finance, children and equity set the size, and φ < q puts the wedge into housing.
Old homes exceed young homes only if γv_i ≥ (Kp − γθ)h^y_i; equity alone never suffices.
