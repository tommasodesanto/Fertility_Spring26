# Independent second review: binding-finance transition

September 9, 2026 UTC. Analytical review of
`overnight_binding_transition_independent.md`, checked against the frozen
household budgets in `simplified_olg_consolidated_theory.tex`. No model runs,
numerical root calculations, browser review, or builds. Only this review file
was written.

## Verdict

**Pass under the stated local regime and external rental-financier convention.**
I found no fatal gap in the owner/renter budget reduction, first-order system,
explicit inverse, fixed-positive-ownership nonlinear construction, endpoint
signs, or common-state comparison. The ownership interval is an analytical
primitive bound, not a numerical example followed by an appeal to continuity.

The following qualifications should accompany incorporation:

1. Apply the nonlinear implicit-function theorem at each **fixed positive**
   owner share and fixed positive logistic scale. The zero-ownership operator
   is a comparison used to bound the inverse. It is not an interior calibration
   with a finite logistic taste location.
2. Specify the topology for nearby inherited states. A sufficient choice
   controls individual financial claims, housing titles, and tenure weights
   uniformly on the fixed compact endowment support, together with the two
   current cohort masses. Nearby aggregate old resources alone do not ensure
   that every individual remains inside the maintained regimes.
3. The explicit ownership-share interval does not supply an explicit numerical
   size for the permitted tax or preference change. Those changes and initial
   state deviations lie in the local neighborhood supplied by the implicit-
   function theorem. A uniform shock-size neighborhood over arbitrary logistic
   scales is not established.
4. Equation (20) certifies slack **competitive and nearby path** housing caps.
   If the compatibility paragraph also asserts slack planner caps, the caps
   must additionally exceed the relevant planner housing demands. This is a
   finite, compatible extra restriction, not a defect in the transition proof.
5. The external intermediary loss-bearing convention is substantive. The
   household and rental-pricing equations do not establish equivalence with a
   domestically owned, fixed-debt rental sector that cannot absorb capital
   losses. No such equivalence or household-welfare conclusion should be added.

## 1. Reconstruction from the original household budgets

With \(\phi=q\), a binding owner mortgage constraint gives
\(a'=-P_t h\). Substitution into the original young budget yields
\[
c+(1-q+q\tau_t)P_t h=w_i+T_t.
\]
The resources entering old age are therefore
\[
z=a'+P_{t+1}h+v_i+T_{t+1}
  =v_i+T_{t+1}+(P_{t+1}-P_t)h.
\]
The face repayment is \(P_t h\); replacing it with a debt amount revalued at
\(P_{t+1}\) would incorrectly remove the capital-gain channel.

In the maintained old regime, both tenure value functions are
\(K\log z+C(p_{t+1})\). The owner housing condition, after imposing its
binding current budget, is exactly
\[
\frac{\alpha}{s}=\frac{L_t}{x}
 -\frac{\beta K(P_{t+1}-P_t)}{z}.
\]
Fertility obeys
\(\vartheta_t/n=\chi/x+\alpha\kappa/s\). A constrained renter instead has
\(a'=0\), current cost \(p_t\), and old resources \(v_i+T_{t+1}\).
Thus equations (4) follow from the frozen constraints without a new borrowing
instrument or a changed wealth definition.

At stationarity \(L=p\), the owner sale value exactly offsets its prior face
debt. Both tenures then have the choices in (2), including \(z_i=v_i\).
Their conditional values coincide. The finance multiplier is strictly
positive precisely under the first inequality of (1). The old estate condition
is \(\omega_B p>q\gamma P\), which is the second inequality of (1).

The replacement and market-clearing formulas in (3) check directly. They
determine the price and cohort mass; they do not independently fix aggregate
external wealth.

For old households generated by the preceding cohort, integration gives
\[
Z_t=V+T_t+(P_t-P_{t-1})\mathbb E[\pi^O_{t-1}h^O_{t-1}].
\]
At the intervention date the actual claims and titles instead give
\(Z_0=\bar a_0+P_0\bar H_0+V+T_0\). Holding those claims and titles fixed
implies \(dZ_0=\bar H_0\,dP_0+dT_0\), not \(dZ_0=dT_0\).

## 2. Independent derivation of the heterogeneous response coefficients

For this subsection define the household coefficient
\[
b_i^{\mathrm f}=\frac{\beta K w_i}{Ev_i}\in(0,q),
\qquad
B_f=\frac{\mathbb E[w_i b_i^{\mathrm f}]}{W}.
\]
Let \(\rho_i=\alpha x_i/s_i\). At the reference,
\[
d\rho_i=dp+(q-b_i^{\mathrm f})\,d\Delta P.
\]
The exact fertility identities give \(Ex_i=c_i+\rho_i h_i\). Combining
this with the owner's current budget and differentiating gives
\[
\frac{dx_i}{x_i}
=\frac{dT}{w_i}-j\zeta
 -\frac{A_h b_i^{\mathrm f}}{E}\frac{d\Delta P}{p}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac{dn_i}{n_i}
=\frac{dT}{w_i}+(1-j)\zeta-\eta\ell
 -\left[\frac{A_h b_i^{\mathrm f}}{E}
       +\eta(q-b_i^{\mathrm f})\right]\frac{d\Delta P}{p}.
\]
At the reference all household housing and fertility quantities are
proportional to \(w_i\). Integration therefore yields exactly the reported
\(d_n\), and differentiation of
\(h_i=\alpha x_i/\rho_i+\kappa\vartheta x_i/(\chi+\kappa\rho_i)\)
yields exactly the reported \(d_h\).

For old households,
\[
d\bar h_t^o=b\xi_t^o-b\ell_t
 +\pi\frac{\gamma h_y}{K}(F_t-F_{t-1}),
\]
including its date-zero version. Thus \(d_o=\gamma h_y/K\) is correct.

Tenure derivatives cancel from first-order real aggregates because the
conditional reference quantities coincide: terms containing
\(d\pi_i(h_i^O-h_i^R)\) or \(d\pi_i(n_i^O-n_i^R)\) vanish. Likewise,
the derivative of the lagged owner quantity is multiplied by the zero
reference price change in old resources. This cancellation is first-order
only; the exact conditional probabilities must be recomputed on the path.

The demographic and housing equations then give (8). The price derivative
and the zero-tax fiscal derivative give (9). In particular, the old initial
capital revaluation enters with the reported \(\pi d_oF_0\) contribution
to housing demand. The convention \(F_{-1}=0\) represents the fixed initial
reference debt price; it is not a requirement that a surprise policy was
anticipated in the preceding period.

## 3. The linear inverse and ownership-share bound

Substitution of exponential modes into (8) gives the exact cubic (10).
At zero ownership the population recurrence has characteristic polynomial
\(r^2-\mathcal A r+k\), where
\[
d-\eta h_y=(1-\eta)a+b>0,
\quad 0<\mathcal A<1,
\quad 0<k<1.
\]
The three quadratic Jury quantities are
\[
1-\mathcal A+k=\eta H_*/d>0,\quad
1+\mathcal A+k>0,\quad 1-k>0.
\]
They establish strictly stable roots. The impulse bound follows from
\(\psi_m=\sum_{j=0}^m r_1^j r_2^{m-j}\); it remains valid for repeated
or complex roots. Summing this bound gives the reported \(C_y\).

The second row recovers \((I-qS)F\) from population and forcing. Its
unique bounded inverse is discounted forward summation, giving \(C_F\).
The backward shifts inserting the fixed initial zeros have norm at most one.
Thus the bounds \(C_0\) and \(C_K\) in (12), and the Neumann bound under
(13), are valid on both convergent and bounded sequence spaces.

All constants depend explicitly on primitives and the closed-form reference
quantities. The expectation \(\mathbb E(w_i^2/v_i)\) is a primitive
distributional moment, not a simulated equilibrium statistic. No numerical
cubic-root calculation is needed.

## 4. Why the same unknown space remains valid at small ownership

Even in the renter comparison limit, the asset price remains an unknown:
rental-intermediary pricing supplies \(p_t=P_t-qP_{t+1}\) at zero tax.
Boundedness eliminates the explosive homogeneous asset-price component.
There is consequently no need to add a price variable or a state dimension
when the owner share becomes positive.

For each chosen \(0<\pi<\pi_*\), fix any \(\sigma_\xi>0\) and set
\[
\bar\xi=\sigma_\xi\log\frac{\pi}{1-\pi}.
\]
This is a finite calibration of the maintained logistic model. The proof uses
the invertible zero-share operator to bound the actual derivative at this
positive-share reference. It does not need to apply a nonlinear implicit-
function theorem at \(\bar\xi=-\infty\).

If a formal probability extension is desired for explanation, writing
\(r=\pi/(1-\pi)\) gives
\[
\pi_t(i)=\frac{r\exp(\Delta W_t(i)/\sigma_\xi)}
                  {1+r\exp(\Delta W_t(i)/\sigma_\xi)},
\]
which has a smooth zero-share extension locally. This observation is not an
additional model assumption and is unnecessary for the fixed-positive-share
construction.

## 5. Nonlinear sequence-space argument and inherited states

Compact positive support and uniform strict regime margins keep consumption,
fertility, effective prices, old resources, and choice Hessians uniformly
inside their admissible regions in a sufficiently small supremum-norm ball.
The conditional choice maps are smooth uniformly over that support. Finite
shifts, integration, and the smooth logistic formula then define a continuously
differentiable residual map on both \(c^4\) and \((\ell^\infty)^4\).
The special initial-old row is finite rank and uses the actual initial state.

At zero tax, the fiscal derivative determines the entire rebate variation,
including an arbitrary fiscal residual. The pricing row similarly determines
the user-cost variation given asset prices and an arbitrary pricing residual.
Eliminating those two rows leaves (11) with arbitrary forcing. The full
derivative is therefore invertible, not merely free of one selected explosive
homogeneous mode.

The implicit-function theorem on \(c\) supplies a convergent path for
convergent exogenous fertility-preference and tax sequences. The theorem on
\(\ell^\infty\) supplies uniqueness within a nearby bounded neighborhood;
the convergent solution belongs to that neighborhood after shrinking the
neighborhoods if needed. No claim of global uniqueness follows.

To make inherited-state language formal, one can fix the compact endowment
law, allow bounded perturbations of conditional old financial claims and
housing titles and of their conditional tenure weights, and keep the initial
cohort masses near their positive reference values. Uniform state control
preserves individual old regime margins. These states enter the date-zero
resource and clearing rows continuously and differentiably. The mean formula
alone is sufficient for aggregate clearing in this regime, but it is not by
itself a certificate of individual feasibility.

## 6. Tax, preference, and endpoint signs

At a stationary tax, all conditional choices still coincide. Combining
replacement, housing clearing, and the rebate equation gives (14) exactly.
Differentiating it yields (15), with a positive local denominator. Thus
\(S_{\rm tax}>0\) is the exact endpoint sign on this branch, and
\(V\ge W\) is sufficient.

Let \(D=\chi+\kappa p=\nu\vartheta W/E\). The claimed zero-share
initial tax response satisfies the useful exact identity
\[
m_\tau=\frac{T'}{d}\frac{S_{\rm tax}}{pD}.
\]
Hence its sign agrees exactly with the endpoint sign. Direct elimination of
the initial rows gives both forcing vectors and their norms in (16) and (17).
The preference margin \(m_\vartheta\) is strictly positive.

The resolvent identity gives
\(\|u_\pi-u_0\|\le2\pi C_0^2C_K\|r\|\). The factors of four in
(18) therefore preserve at least half each strictly positive reference
initial-response margin. Under \(S_{\rm tax}>0\), neither forcing norm
in the denominator vanishes, and \(\pi_*\) is strictly positive. Strict
derivative signs imply the stated signs for sufficiently small finite changes.

The zero-tax preference derivative of stationary price is correct. Increasing
the fertility preference raises price while reducing
\(\alpha W/E+\gamma V/K\), so housing per stationary household pair falls
and the limiting cohort mass rises. A small preference decline has the reverse
endpoint effect, although endpoint fertility remains replacement fertility.

## 7. Resetting policy on a nearby baseline transition

At the intervention date, both scenarios must inherit the same young and old
cohort masses and the same actual old financial claims, titles, and tenure
composition. The common inherited object is not old resources evaluated at
each scenario's new price. The latter changes through capital revaluation.
Use equation (6) after every reset, and the same subsequent fertility-
preference sequence in both scenarios.

A nearby convergent baseline and its inherited states lie in the local
parameter neighborhood. Continuity of the policy derivative preserves the
strict initial tax sign there. The limits satisfy the stationary equations
at the same limiting preference, so the strictly positive stationary tax
derivative also persists locally. This establishes the claimed initial
fertility and limiting population comparisons. It does not establish
all-date fertility ordering, recovery of the original population, or welfare.

The product identity for relative cohort mass follows directly from the
demographic equation and common initial young mass. Positive convergent
limits imply the stated limiting cumulative log fertility gap. Absolute
summability of the individual log gaps is not needed or established.

## 8. Compatibility scope

Conditions (19a) and (19b) have the claimed implications. The stronger
future-income restrictions can be satisfied on heterogeneous compact support
for every fixed finite \(\beta\), after choosing a positive replacement
price. Finite caps satisfying (20) then have strict reference margins that
persist on the local path. The construction retains the exact restriction
\(\phi=q\); it is not an open result over arbitrary financed shares.

For an explicit additional certificate of slack planner caps, let
\[
n_{\max}=\frac{w_{\max}}{\nu W},\qquad
S_F=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\gamma}(a+b).
\]
In addition to (20), it is sufficient to choose the renter cap above
\(\kappa n_{\max}+S_F\) and \(H_*\), and choose the owner cap larger
still. The first bound covers every fixed-fertility planner young house;
the second covers its old houses and both ages of the uncapped joint optimum.
All these quantities are finite primitive functions. This additional cap
certificate is only needed when asserting uncapped-planner compatibility;
the transition result itself already passes with (20).
