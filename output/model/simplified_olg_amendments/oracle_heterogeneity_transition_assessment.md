## What changes economically

Young recipients and old donors can be different income–wealth groups. The earlier proof forced a single type to have little enough liquidity to be constrained when young but enough housing to downsize when old. The revised argument separates these requirements. It also bounds the young household's current housing value without requiring its own future retention constraint to be slack.

The stationary result retains a nondegenerate compact distribution of entrant income and wealth, positive property taxes and mixed tenure. Aggregate replacement can be sustained by owners despite renters remaining below replacement. Initial entrant wealth remains exogenous as in the original model.

## Result and limits

| Part | Claimed result | Boundary of the claim |
|---|---|---|
| Stationary proposition | Explicit sufficient conditions establish at least one positive level-stationary equilibrium with a compensated housing improvement; every equilibrium inside the specified interval has the improvement. | No exclusion of equilibria outside the interval. Conditions involve selected type sets, analytical household bounds, and a chosen price interval, not one short scalar inequality. |
| Dated proposition | Given an equilibrium path and its actual inherited old state, explicit resource and price tests derive a compensated improving housing transfer at that date. | Does not derive the price path or prove existence/convergence after the fertility or policy shock. It uses endogenous dated prices and state variables. |
| Carry-forward extension | Sufficient conditions propagate suitable old households from their earlier choices along a zero-tax path. | Assumes a positive price floor and a lower bound on consecutive price ratios. Those bounds have not been derived from initial states and shocks. |
| Large-decline obstruction | A sufficiently large permanent fertility-preference decline makes population shrink faster than finite housing caps can accommodate the fully occupied fixed stock. | An obstruction for that shock region and the exact-clearing model, not proof that the moderate transition of interest fails. Vacancy or another physical closure would be a substantive extension. |

## Restrictions to assess before adoption

- The new lower fertility bracket is aggregate, but conservative: selected owners alone must contribute more than replacement at the low-price endpoint. Ignoring all other births can be costly when selected owners are a small group.
- The upper fertility bracket also deliberately overstates possible housing/fertility for each type. Its economic tightness is not established merely because its ingredients are explicit.
- The nonempty-family construction keeps nu fixed, but selects high resources relative to liquid wealth, sufficiently small beta, a positive sufficiently small tax, and finite taste parameters satisfying bounds. Small beta is a device for that construction, not a formal restriction imposed on every application of Proposition 1. This is analytical nonemptiness, not evidence that the conditions encompass the quantitatively relevant region.
- Calling the chosen price endpoints "primitive" is imprecise. They are auxiliary bounds selected and checked using primitives; they are not original preference/endowment parameters or solved equilibrium prices.
- The saved taxed reference satisfies the revised dated household test. This does not show it satisfies all stationary existence inequalities.
- Date-by-date improvements cannot automatically be combined into a policy along the entire path. Fertility and eventual population effects remain separate from the fixed-fertility welfare argument.

## Suggested discussion order

1. Decide whether separating young recipient types from old donor types gives the right simple economic explanation.
2. Inspect the actual young/old restrictions and whether the stationary existence conditions are too conservative for the examples and calibrated mechanism.
3. Return to the transition objective: distinguish the useful dated test from the still-missing equilibrium path argument. Assess the fixed-stock/finite-cap obstruction before attempting a global theorem.

## Independent review

Two max-reasoning reviewers performed distinct bounded analytical checks. The stationary household bounds, endogenous tenure lower bound, simultaneous rebate/fertility existence argument and nonempty-family construction passed. The dated young benchmark, exact old cutoff and restoration of gross saving also passed. The carry-forward argument needs an explicit initial-state qualification: its parameter and price bounds must cover the young-age decision that generated the initial old cohort, or initial donor eligibility must be checked directly. An unspecified stationary initial cohort is not sufficient on its own.
