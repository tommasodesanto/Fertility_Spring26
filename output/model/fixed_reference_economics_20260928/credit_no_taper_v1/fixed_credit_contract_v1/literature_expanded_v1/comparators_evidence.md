# Two unsecured-credit comparators

Read-only literature check, September 30. No model adoption, edits, or solves.

**Maxted, Laibson, and Moll, QJE (2025), “Present Bias Amplifies the Household Balance-Sheet Channels of Macroeconomic Policy.”** Published Section IV.A.4 fixes liquid wealth at \(b\ge-1/3\), one-third of annual permanent income. The model has a common permanent-income normalization and stochastic transitory income; this is a constant cap, not a cap moving with current income. [Published article, Sections II.B and IV.A](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/1/691/7754885).

Appendix A.1, Table 4 (printed p.45) attributes \(-1/3\) to the 2016 SCF. Appendix A.2 (p.46) reports mean credit-limit/permanent-income ratio **0.35**. Data ratios use each household’s after-tax “normal income”; mean after-tax permanent income is **$95,718**. Sample: homeowners possessing a credit card, head aged 25–61, head or spouse in labor force; income trimmed at 1st/99th percentiles and home-value/income below 95th percentile. It captures 74% of homeowners aged 25–61. Thus the empirical object is credit capacity, not the lower tail of observed net assets. [Publisher article and supplementary appendix, A.1–A.2](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/1/691/7754885).

Credit-card borrowing pays \(r_t+10.3\%\), versus saving return \(r_t\); the spread uses 2015–17 credit-card/Treasury rates. Mortgage debt is separate and amortizes at 3.5% annually. Borrowing is a hard bound without an endogenous default-price schedule. These objects coexist with cash-out refinancing and present bias. The cap’s empirical anchor is useful for a constant real \(D\), but its population is homeowners, and its annual-income units require conversion; four-year model periods do not imply multiplying a debt stock by four. [Published article, II.B and IV.A.3–4](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/1/691/7754885).

**Herkenhoff, Phillips, and Cohen-Cole, ReStud (2024), qualified supporting evidence.** Publication verified: 91(5), pp.2832–2877. Numerical specification below is verified only in the author-linked April 25, 2023 manuscript, not the published full text. [Publisher record](https://academic.oup.com/restud/article-abstract/91/5/2832/7444948).

Its quarterly model fixes \(b'\ge\underline b=-0.66\) in model-good units. Table 10 (p.36) names the 2004 SCF median unused-limit/income target: model **0.12**, data **0.15**. Surrounding prose (p.34) instead calls the target unused-limit/total-limit utilization: unresolved definition discrepancy. Default is available, debt prices depend on repayment probabilities, and effective individual credit caps can be tighter than the common floor (p.37). Annual risk-free rate is 4%; bankruptcy credit re-access probability is 0.025 quarterly (pp.32–35). Its empirical credit-access ratio includes HELOCs alongside unsecured revolving credit (pp.9–10), preventing unqualified use as renter unsecured capacity. It supports measuring limits separately from balances, but **does not supply a portable \(D=0.66\) or income fraction**. [Author manuscript, pp.9–10, 27–37](https://facultynew.tuck.dartmouth.edu/uploads/gordonPhillips/publications/credit_access_job_finding_4_25_23.pdf).
