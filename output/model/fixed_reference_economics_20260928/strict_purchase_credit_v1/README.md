# Fixed-price strict-purchase credit diagnostic

This is an experimental **partial-equilibrium diagnostic**, not a new general equilibrium or a calibration. The strict wealth-only origination rule remains: a buyer may use liquid wealth held at the start of the period and proceeds from selling the current home, but not current income, to meet the down payment. The sole policy input changed between cases is the financed share \(\phi\), from **0.8 to 0.9 for both buyers and owner-stayers**. The interest timing and budget \(b'=R(b+S-Q)+y-c-K\) are unchanged. All ten calibrated coordinates, entry distribution, preferences, targets, weights, grids, pension and tax rules, and other economic inputs are held at the completed strict-80 case.

The housing price is fixed at **0.7152515073815459**, the completed strict-80 equilibrium price. The physical housing-supply coefficient is fixed at **\(H_0=6.778473404808042\)**, and initial physical population is **\(N_0=1\)**. There is no price root, housing-market clearing, or fertility normalization in the new solve. Birth replacement and the housing-market residual are measured and reported as diagnostic outcomes. Native stationary cohort, transition-accounting, occupied-renter, feasibility, and PAYGO checks remain active.

The driver runs exactly three fresh one-price lifecycle solves: a strict-80 replay, a strict-90 case, and an exact strict-90 repeat. Each case must produce the native 14-row target fit, 31-row parameter table, and 17 standard plots. The 80 replay must match the completed strict-80 target table within \(10^{-10}\); the 90 repeat must agree exactly on the native tables, closure, and plot hashes. The original strict-purchase engine is mounted unchanged. The copied input receipts in `input/strict80/` are byte-identical to the completed strict-80 packet. The driver saves source variants and diffs for the fixed-price observer and phase driver.

Torch job **19006254** was independently confirmed **RUNNING** on `cs607` in a scheduler snapshot around **00:14 New York, October 2**; no completed case was reported in that snapshot. The launch requests **one CPU, 24 GiB, and 20 minutes** with a 300-second per-case solve limit and no automatic retry. After collection, run `python3 build_readout.py` to validate the three-case result and generate complete comparison tables; it does not run the model.
