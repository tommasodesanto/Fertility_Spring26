# Experimental purchase-rule calibrations at 80% financing

Submitted October 2, 2026: exploratory smoke array **19009130** (hard chain 0, quarter chain 24), followed by dependency-gated 48-chain production array **19009131**. At submission both arrays were pending; this note does not claim smoke passage or production startup. The staged archive and Slurm IDs are authenticated in `deployment/stage_verification.json`. Root source files covered by `manifest.json` are frozen for these jobs.

This packet searches two distinct purchase rules using the same 2007 normalized stationary calibration contract. Chains 0–23 use the hard closing rule, \(A\geq0.2Q\). Chains 24–47 use the quarter-saving rule, \(A+0.25[b'-(A-Q)]\geq0.2Q\). \(A\) is financial wealth plus net proceeds from selling the previous home, \(Q\) is the chosen home's price, and \(b'\) is the end-of-period financial balance. Both retain the original budget, interest timing, 80% owner debt ceiling, no unsecured renter credit, 120×9 state grid, nonnegative mean-preserving entrants, normalized population \(N_0=1\), a renewal-clearing price, and housing supply coefficient derived from room demand. Both isolated engines enforce a buyer's end-of-period net estate solvency in the Bellman saving choice. The quarter-saving rule intentionally permits an owner moving from a negative closing balance when one quarter of net saving covers the required equity. Neither rule is adopted as the paper baseline by this experiment.

The complete target and weight fingerprints are in `plan.json` and `manifest.json`. Ten coordinates remain free for ten scored moments; four additional rows are normalization or validation. The parenthood-room floor \(h_P\) has the previously authorized search bounds [0.1,2.6]. All other bounds are unchanged. The soft-rule search supplies only starts: eight current provisional soft-search checkpoints per arm are explicitly **unverified starting points**, never reference results. The other starts cover the verified center, four floor anchors and wider deterministic neighbors. `soft_provisional_seeds.json` records the exact source-file hashes.

Forty-eight independent Nelder–Mead chains are planned, 24 per rule. Each chain has one core, 24 GiB, at most four hours from actual start, at most 250 objective calls, and 15 minutes reserved for a fresh selected-point native postcheck. The hard cap is 12,000 candidate evaluations; at observed multi-minute solve times, the actual count will be much lower. Search writes `latest_completed.json`, `best_so_far.json`, `cases.json`, and `search_completed.json` per chain. Two separate one-case exploratory smoke jobs (one per rule) must pass before the 48-chain array begins. `deployment/launch_torch.sh` retains the 14-row fit, 31-row parameter and 17-plot native postcheck. Native selected arrays are preserved in `postcheck/selected_postcheck/phase_b_ge/selected_repeat/stage/solution_arrays.npz`; this variant adds `distribution.g_pre` and the other directly observed distribution arrays alongside `g`, saving and housing policy arrays, entry masses and birth hazards. Selected-root CSVs and plots remain in the parallel `selected_root` directory.

The original frozen calibration source is authenticated. The two isolated experimental engines are loaded before the integration imports, and all 157 engine files are pinned in `engine_pins.json`; the runtime checks that the executed household, kernels and solver modules come from the intended arm. `deployment/stage_torch.sh` stages a source-only archive. `deployment/submit_torch.sh` submits the smoke pair and dependent production array after lead review. Smoke results use `smoke_chain_0` and `smoke_chain_24`, separate from production chain directories. No automatic restart or silent target/bound change is configured. The selected result must pass its fresh postcheck and target/parameter/closure checks before it is called a fitted candidate; optimizer convergence and identification rank remain unverified unless separately shown.

Experimental changes relative to the normalized soft-rule calibration are: (1) closing eligibility under either hard or quarter-saving rule, (2) the buyer's end-of-period net estate solvency floor in the Bellman choice, and (3) the previously authorized \(h_P\) upper search bound 2.6. Earnings, entrant distributions, taxes, transfers, preferences, all target definitions/weights, and interest timing are unchanged. Any 80% to 100% financed-share policy experiment must be run separately at a verified fitted point and must distinguish one-period from permanent changes.
