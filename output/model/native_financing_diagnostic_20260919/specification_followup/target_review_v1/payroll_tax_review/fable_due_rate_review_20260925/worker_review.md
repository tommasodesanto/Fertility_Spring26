# Independent worker review of the Fable DUE-rate finding

This note is an evidence and wording check for lead review. It is not a new model recommendation or author decision.

- **Verified primary-source distinction:** the Feb. 2025 NBER version calls the input a “payroll tax rate” but footnote 29 identifies OECD's “Average income tax rate.” Its prose explicitly ties 17.9% to 2012–16; the source text and footnote were extracted from the PDF binary saved by the authorized WebFetch call. The Dec. 2025 paper's own table/prose/footnote on pp. 27 and 31 were also read from the cached DUE source by the lead.
- **Stale-table status is an inference:** the older window (2012–16) and the Dec. 2025 prose window (2005–09) differ, while the later table retains 0.179. That makes a stale table the best explanation, but the paper does not state the reason or which number entered the reported solve. Phrase it as a version-consistency inference.
- **The 15.6 denominator remains unresolved:** the current OECD gross-wage measure averages 16.954% in 2005–09; dividing each year's average income-tax rate by (1+) the employer SSC share of gross wages yields 15.583%. That arithmetic is compatible with a labour-cost denominator. Since the OECD decomposition series was not retrieved and the later footnote simply says “Measure: Income tax,” do not present the labour-cost explanation as verified source definition.
- **Government accounting claim needs precise wording:** the DUE text says payroll-tax revenue goes toward pension transfers and wasteful government spending. Its displayed equilibrium conditions (p. 20) do not include a government-budget equation, and the pension benefit has no discovered calibration row/sentence in the bounded pages. This establishes that the cited passages do not specify a PAYGO pension-only balance. Absence of a displayed budget condition in those pages does not prove no fiscal rule exists elsewhere, nor does it identify the split of receipts between pensions and residual spending.
- **Avoid a mechanical “must add tax” recommendation:** 17.9% is not an OASI contribution rate, so it should not be relabeled as a pension-only levy. Adopting a broad earnings-tax level would require a fiscal design that states pension benefits and non-pension government spending. It does not imply mechanically adding 17.9% to the 8.751% proxy; these rates have different meanings, vintages, and bases.
- **Interpret 8.751% narrowly:** it is a transparent contribution-rate-times-effective-base proxy for 2007 OASI on this model's uncapped household gross earnings. It is a candidate pension-financing contribution object, not an estimate of the total earnings tax burden or a complete mapping of worker disposable income. The model's combined-side attribution and household earnings proxy remain approximations.
