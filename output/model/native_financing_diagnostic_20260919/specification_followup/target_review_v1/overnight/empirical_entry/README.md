# Entry wealth units check

This packet replicates the July 18–24 PSID entry proxy and compares it with RP/spouse gross labor earnings on the identical sample. The calculation is descriptive and read-only: no model solve, active code, or target was changed.

The July sample reproduces exactly: 1,835 reference-person family-years and 1,346 people, ages 18–24, 1984–2019 waves, childless (`RELCHIREP == 0`), renter (`HOMEOWN == 2`), `INCFAMR > $1,000`, positive `IW`, alive in the interview year, with finite `NETWORTH2R`. The original five weighted-bin means of `NETWORTH2R / INCFAMR` are `−2.22253, −0.05264, 0.10424, 0.35157, 3.10345`; their weights, overall mean `0.258856`, and median `0.098518` reproduce the July source values. The overall-mean person-cluster bootstrap uses 499 draws and seed 20260715; its SE is `0.125966`, matching the saved July estimate.

The PSID shelf labels `EARNINDRRC` as “RP/SP's combined earnings (real USD 2022), tax year”; it labels `INCFAMR` as “FU's total family income (real USD 2022), tax year” and `year` as “Survey year.” These exact labels are saved in `source_variable_labels.csv`. The project’s aggregate wealth-earnings builder reads `EARNINDRRC` and explicitly names it `gross_labor_earnings` ([audit_aggregate_wealth_earnings_ratio.R](/Users/tommasodesanto/Desktop/Projects/Fertility/Fertility_Spring26/code/data/psid_followup_mar2026/audit_aggregate_wealth_earnings_ratio.R:89)); the income-process builder identifies it as RP/spouse combined real gross earnings ([build_psid_income_between_within.R](/Users/tommasodesanto/Desktop/Projects/Fertility/Fertility_Spring26/code/data/psid_followup_mar2026/build_psid_income_between_within.R:101)). Thus the labor concept comes from the project builder, while the shelf label itself says combined earnings. Both income variables are tax-year measures on survey-year rows; no survey/tax-year shift is applied. All 1,835 sample rows have a finite earnings value, but 67 are zero and 26 are positive at or below $1,000. Applying the existing `> $1,000` denominator eligibility rule leaves 1,742 family-years / 1,296 people and 95.37% of the sample’s IW mass. These exclusions are part of the gross-denominator comparison, not changes to the July sample.

For the ratio-of-weighted-sums estimand, the gross-labor denominator raises the same-support wealth ratio by 29.8% relative to family income. That is a denominator change in a separate candidate estimand; it does not establish that the July decision was wrong. The weighted mean of row-level ratios is a different object from the ratio of weighted sums: on the all-July sample they are `0.25886` and `0.36891`, respectively. At row level the gross ratio has a very large mean because low positive earnings and high wealth generate a long upper tail; its median is much closer to the family-income median. A denominator restricted to earners above $1,000 also conditions on current earnings.

Weighted Spearman rank correlations between nonhousing net worth and income are 0.330 for family income (1,835 rows), 0.282 for gross earnings including zeros (1,835 rows), and 0.280 for the `> $1,000` gross-denominator sample (1,742 rows). These are descriptive sample associations; no cutoff or pass/fail interpretation is attached. The full weighted earnings-tercile × wealth-quintile table is in `joint_earnings_tercile_wealth_quintile.csv`; quintiles and terciles are re-ranked within the common eligible sample. Cell entries are shares of total IW mass.

The weighted Spearman calculation uses family-year IW weights. Values are sorted into exact-value groups so ties share the weighted midpoint rank \((W_{<x}+0.5W_{=x})/W\); the reported correlation is the IW-weighted Pearson correlation of those ranks. The B15 model correlation uses this same rank and correlation convention, with joint model entry probabilities in place of IW weights and ties at identical wealth-grid or income-state values grouped exactly.

The group-level output makes the distinction between levels and ratios explicit. `group_level_normalization.csv` reports weighted mean nonhousing wealth and family income within both wealth-ranked and income-ranked quintiles, their quotient, and wealth divided by the full-sample mean family income. Those are level normalizations; they are not means of household-level wealth/income ratios.

## B15 model-input mapping (no solve)

The frozen B15 period-income constructor uses ρ = 0.73459349, persistent innovation SD `0.48383082`, and 15 persistent states with weighted mean (z=1). Using the new bundle’s age-18 `P.income[0,0] = 2.36630023`, four-year period, and payroll tax `0.179`, age-18 annual gross income is `0.72055427 × z`, spanning `0.03883–8.06596` model units. The old checkpoint’s pre-choice conditional wealth matrix is mapped to the B15 ranks by the frozen overlap rule. The resulting wealth marginal remains `0.18651968` with maximum absolute gap `5.6e-17` and L1 gap `2.6e-16`; this verifies preservation of levels only.

Dividing the preserved B15 conditional wealth distribution by B15 annual gross income gives a pooled implied ratio mean `0.25447`, near the July row-ratio mean `0.25886`, but a weighted median of `0` versus the July median `0.09852`. For descriptive scale, the empirical median’s person-bootstrap SE is `0.01321`; model uncertainty is not estimated, so this is not a statistical test. The model-imposed weighted Spearman correlation between entry wealth and B15 annual gross income is `0.09774`, compared with `0.27993` for gross earnings above $1,000 in the PSID sample. The model value is generated by interval-overlap rank coupling and the inherited wealth marginal, not estimated. This compares model age-18 entrants with PSID family-years ages 18–24 and should be read with that sample mismatch in mind. Implied conditional mean wealth/income ratios range from `0.119` to `0.533` across the 15 states, while the July ratio-node mapping assigns the same conditional ratio mean `0.25886` at each state. The end states have tiny probability mass (each approximately `0.006%`); most mass is in the interior. Applying the original cumulative-weight-cut rule to the discrete B15 wealth-grid/state mass gives ratio-bin means `−2.183, −0.062, 0.021, 0.391, 3.052`, compared with the July nodes `−2.223, −0.053, 0.104, 0.352, 3.103`. The B15 bins carry unequal weight when an atom crosses a cut (the second and third bins contain 16.2% and 22.1%). A close pooled mean does not imply that the old level marginal reproduces the July ratio distribution or its conditional mapping.

The primary bootstrap uncertainty in the original builder is for the overall mean ratio. Node SE rows in `bootstrap_summary.csv` are supplemental fixed-original-bin means: each resample retains the original five bin assignments rather than re-estimating weighted cut points. They are not bootstrap SEs for re-estimated quantile nodes and are not used for inference here.

The saved new B15 mapping preserves old income-rank intervals and couples their wealth distributions to B15 persistent ranks; iid risk is independent conditional on persistent rank. The correlation is imposed by this diagnostic construction, not estimated from the sample. The comparison is not a calibration or an adoption recommendation. It uses beginning/pre-choice liquid wealth in model units and annual gross income at age 18; it does not convert assets to PSID dollars or validate the four-year information timing.
