# Common-scale candidate for entry wealth (diagnostic only)

This folder constructs a proposed entry-wealth law while leaving the signed-off July law and every running model bundle untouched. The candidate is ω_i = NETWORTH2R_i / Ebar_wave, where Ebar_wave is a single IW-weighted RP gross-earnings mean shared by all eligible working-age reference persons in survey wave `year`. It is not NETWORTH2R_i divided by the entrant's own earnings. The candidate is not adopted and has not been run through a household solve or equilibrium.

## Empirical construction

The base remains the exact July Block 2 PSID sample: 1,835 reference-person family-years / 1,346 people, ages 18–24, childless, renters, alive in the interview year, `INCFAMR > $1,000`, positive `IW`, and finite `NETWORTH2R`, in the 14 represented survey waves spanning 1984–2019. All 1,835 rows have finite `EARNINDRRC`; 67 have zero and 26 have positive earnings no greater than $1,000. None is excluded for low or zero own earnings. `EARNINDRRC` is the RP/spouse combined earnings variable in real 2022 USD and is labeled as a tax-year measure on survey-year rows; the wave key is the survey year, with no added tax-year shift.

For each wave, Ebar uses the aggregate wealth builder's core support and earnings rules: reference persons (`RELTOHEAD_ == 10`), age 18–65 for the denominator, positive IW, finite `NETWORTHR` in the age-18–85 working-population universe, finite nonnegative `EARNINDRRC`, and zero earnings retained. The candidate denominator additionally applies the task's explicit alive-in-wave condition. The authoritative aggregate builder itself uses waves 2005–2019 and does not filter `DEATHYEAR`; the candidate extends the same age, wealth, earnings, and weight rules back to the earlier July entrant waves so all 1,835 entrants can receive a same-wave normalizer. This is a support extension, not an exact reuse of the builder's aggregate sample. Of the entrants, 1,398 family-years fall in 2005–2019 and 437 in earlier waves. The alive rule drops four denominator records in 2005–2019 relative to the builder; across the eight represented builder waves the resulting Ebar differs by 0–0.0243% per wave. Candidate Ebar ranges from $73,353.92 to $84,210.08 (2022 USD).

The five wealth bins inside each of three pooled `EARNINDRRC` terciles are ranked on ω, not raw wealth. Exact tied values share a bin according to their IW-weighted midpoint rank; actual probability shares are therefore retained in `entry_nodes_3x5.csv`, rather than set to one-third or one-fifth. For example, the first earnings tercile's five node probabilities are 6.684%, 5.164%, 8.264%, 6.652%, and 6.660%. The complete 15-cell table reports each probability and its conditional mean.

The IW-weighted sample mean is E[ω] = 0.185776. Multiplying the 15 conditional means by their actual cell probabilities reproduces 0.185776 to numerical precision. This estimand is distinct from the pooled ratio of weighted sums, sum(IW NETWORTH2R) / sum(IW EARNINDRRC) = 0.486502; both are retained in `candidate_metrics.csv` so the wave-normalized mean is not mistaken for pooled W/Y.

The weighted Spearman correlation between ω and gross earnings is 0.28250 in the row-level July sample and 0.28360 after replacing wealth with its 3×5 cell mean. This is descriptive. The node replacement has weighted MSE 1.25274, RMSE 1.11926, and MAE 0.30254 in ω units. MSE is 79.12% of the original weighted variance, so the 3×5 conditional means explain 20.88% of that variance. No acceptance cutoff is imposed.

## Frozen B15 mapping and renter-floor resource check

The saved B15 mapping preserves the 15 empirical node probabilities and the B15 persistent-income marginal. It overlaps the observed earnings-tercile rank intervals with the B15 z-state rank intervals, then distributes each overlap across the five wealth nodes using their actual conditional probabilities within that tercile. This is a discretized coupling assumption. A separate finer rank-overlap audit finds that the product-within-tercile rule assigns 0.2774% of total probability to nine node/state combinations with zero mass in the finer rank-coupled microdata support (`discretization_support.csv`). Thus the mapped joint distribution does not exactly preserve every within-tercile wealth/income association.

The conversion uses the actual serialized parameters loaded by the overnight run, with both frozen utility adapters and B15 income/debt/fiscal bindings applied without a household or equilibrium solve (`actual_frozen_parameters.json`). Mean annual gross earnings per working household are 1.000000 model unit. The 12 working-age profile values average to one; the age-18 value is 0.720554. Payroll tax is 0.179, period length four years, and age-18 after-tax period income is 2.366300. Consequently b = ω to numerical precision: the 15 candidate wealth nodes span [-0.547485, 1.566825], with mean 0.185776. Actual R_gross is 1.02^4 = 1.08243216. These objects are identical across the two utility bindings.

The actual parameter object enables age-specific survival, equal to one throughout working ages and below one in retirement. The analytic B15 working-age masses equal the saved selected-B seven-state masses within 1e-12. No candidate B15 equilibrium has been solved. An earlier mapper mistakenly read source defaults (a different age profile, 1.04 annual return and disabled survival) instead of the serialized seed. Its scale 1.112608, mean wealth 0.206696 and resource calculations are superseded and preserved in `preliminary_rejected/source_defaults_v1/`; this correction changes only the candidate readout, never the submitted model bundle.

The first pass counted states with R_gross b + Y <= 0 as infeasible. That interpretation is withdrawn: the model allows unsecured debt rollover. The actual inherited parameters, checked after both utility bindings, have lambda_d=0, taper ages 42–62, and zero transfer floors; at age 18 the next-age taper is one and D_next=0, so the renter floor is max(min(b,0), b_grid[0]) after grid clipping. The overnight B_floor and B_shares adapters both impose the childless eqscale state with cbar=hbar=0; the parenthood-specific housing requirement is inactive for childless entrants. Thus the solver’s current-period gate uses slack R_gross b + P.income[0,0]z - renter_floor, not the sign of R_gross b + Y.

Using the saved candidate joint law and exact frozen selected-B grid interpolation support, this gate slack is strictly positive for all 85 positive-mass node/state pairs and all 119 positive-mass neighboring-grid/state points. Its minimum is 0.08239 at continuous candidate nodes and 0.07192 on interpolation support. Therefore the candidate mass passing this entrant current-period resource/floor gate is 100%. This only checks the solver’s current-period dead-state resource gate for the childless entrant; it does not establish Bellman continuation viability, optimal allocations, later child states, or equilibrium validity. This aggregate check does not certify reported policy levels at every state. The code trace, exact parameter values, and aggregate support table are in `renter_slack_correction.json` and `renter_slack_grid_support.csv`.

The current mapped share with R_gross b + Y <= 0 is 1.7955%, retained only as a cash-only sign statistic in `cash_only_resource_sign_decomposition.csv`. It is not an infeasible share because inherited unsecured debt can roll over. The previous direct July cash-sign calculation used the wrong return and is removed from the current metrics; its superseded evidence remains in `preliminary_rejected/source_defaults_v1/`. No raw-person data reread was needed for this correction.

The model-imposed weighted Spearman between candidate wealth and B15 z is 0.24089 under exact weighted midpoint tie ranks. The saved inherited reference-level mapping gives 0.09774 under its own rank coupling. The July row-level correlation is 0.28250. These are descriptive comparisons across a PSID age-18–24 family-year sample and age-18 model entrants; none is a threshold or statistical pass/fail test.
