## 2. What blocks a defensible calibration

Four things block it. The geographic scope, because it sets four target values. The earnings measurement contract, because the current numbers are a diagnostic artifact. The period-and-grid approximation, because fertility outcomes are not converged at 15 states, so no refit under the new process is interpretable yet. And the housing-block device, because the completed search documents a conflict the current architecture cannot resolve by parameter choice.

That conflict is the main new finding. Across the extracted proposals, $H_0$ and $\chi$ move ownership and rooms together: cheaper housing raises both. The proposal nearest both housing levels, with $\chi=1.13$ and $H_0=5.9$, gets ownership 0.59 and rooms 5.42, but its recent-parent ownership gap is −0.10 against +0.16 and its mean first-birth age is 27.4, so its loss is 2241. The selected proposal, with $\chi=0.95$ and $H_0=10.2$, reverses this: the recent-parent gap is exact and first-birth age 26.7, but ownership is 0.48 and rooms 6.68. Every proposal below loss 480 has $h_P$ at 2.3 and $\beta$ within 0.005 of 0.99. The original benchmark shows the same trade at a milder level. The economics: at a price that keeps rooms near the data, the deposit binds hardest for households that just became parents, so parents rent and births are delayed; at a price that lets parents buy, everyone holds too much space. The lifecycle overlay adds that rooms overshoot by about 0.8 at all working ages and by 1.5 to 1.8 after 66, and ownership is 0.10 to 0.14 too low at 26–45 and 0.11 to 0.21 too high after 78.

Compensation to expect in any refit: $H_0$ against $\chi$ on ownership and rooms; both against the recent-parent gap and timing rows; $\psi$, $\xi$ and the two $\kappa$ on childlessness and timing; $\beta$ against $\theta_0,\theta_1$ on the wealth level and the old tail, with $\theta_1$ weakly identified in the September 7 Jacobian. A finite search cannot prove any row unreachable, and a bound hit is not proof of conflict; the frontier reading above is a documented pattern, not a theorem.

Classification. Empirical quantities to estimate: the earnings process and age profile; the four housing rows under the chosen scope; the CPS income gradient if a child cost is ever proposed. Structural parameters to fit: the nine coordinates plus $\psi$ normalization, plus $\chi_1$ only inside Experiment 1. Numerical approximations to validate: the period proxy, the income grid, the entry construction, the non-monotone owner-entry segments already visible in the control. Provisionally fixed, with the contract line stated: $\kappa_H$, $\lambda_d=0$, $\phi$, gross estate valuation, static supply, closure deferred.

## 3. Two experiments

**Can the completed search answer first?** Partly, at zero solves. Its per-case file is local and holds all 13 fit rows and 9 parameters for 89 valid proposals. Tabulating the frontier across ownership, rooms, the recent-parent gap and first-birth age, and the parameter movements behind it, should precede any new solve; I read 30 proposals by hand and two in full. It cannot answer the block question, since every proposal shares the cap-and-uniform-premium architecture, nor the grid question, since every proposal uses 15 states.

**Experiment 1. Family ownership premium, bounded refit, original earnings process.** Owner services become $\chi(m)\,(h-\bar h(m))$ with $\chi(m)=\chi_0+\chi_1\mathbf 1\{m>0\}$, a one-line change in the frozen-source path in the same style as the rental-cost patch, with $\chi_1=0$ bitwise-off. Held fixed: the frozen September-14 source plus this patch, the original earnings process, the cap at six, $\phi$, $\kappa_H$, $\lambda_d=0$, all 12 scored rows, weights and fingerprint, the 2.1 normalization, the nine bounds unchanged. Reestimated: the nine coordinates plus $\chi_1\ge0$, seeded at the retained point, twelve joint proposals with the overnight design, two exact repetitions of the selected point. Outcome: the full 13-row table and the four-row frontier against the search's frontier at comparable loss; the recent-parent gap is the identifying row for $\chi_1$, freeing $H_0$ for rooms. Interpretation: this is a structural hypothesis that families value tenure security more, not a fit residual; if it does not relieve the four-row conflict, the device is rejected and the conflict is attributed to the deposit-versus-price channel itself. Stop: after twelve proposals; a positive result requires at least one proposal with ownership at or above 0.60, rooms at or below 6.0, recent-parent gap at or above 0.12 and first-birth age at or below 26.5, at a loss below the benchmark. Cost: one switches-off control that must reproduce 179.298, twelve proposals, two repetitions: 15 stationary evaluations.

**Experiment 2. Period proxy and grid convergence under the proposed earnings form.** Held fixed: the nine retained coordinates, the current annual estimates as inputs, prices solved, $\psi$ re-normalized, full target system. Varied: the moment-matched period proxy discretized on 15, 27 and 45 joint states; entry rebuilt by the native rule and its wealth distribution reported. Outcome: the 13-row table across grids. Interpretation: if any scored row moves by more than its recorded bootstrap standard error between 27 and 45 states, the 15-state selected point is not a usable object for specification comparison and the earnings refit must wait for the finer grid at its higher cost per solve. Stop: three evaluations plus one repetition of the 45-state case. Cost: 4 stationary evaluations.

Total 19 of 32 stationary evaluations; fixed-price evaluations are nearly exhausted after today's fourteen, and neither experiment needs them. Cohort checks on the selected points come after the results.
