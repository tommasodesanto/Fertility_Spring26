# Lead assessment of the second Claude Max review

The first-party Max/Fable review completed in 460 seconds. Its final memo is
supporting advice, not an adopted specification. The lead retains the author's
priority: specification and credible calibration before policy interpretation.

Accepted direction: a minimal baseline, conventional persistent-plus-transitory
earnings, one coherent income concept, explicit period/grid/entry approximation,
a retained hard tenure/space proxy while fit is diagnosed, and a distinction
between external measurement, structural estimation and numerical validation.
The saved 89 valid proposals are useful descriptive evidence before more solves.

The following claims or proposals are not accepted:

- The sentence that the current architecture cannot resolve the housing conflict
  by parameter choice overstates an adaptive finite search. Reading 30 proposals
  by hand is not a controlled response map or an exhaustive fit frontier. The
  later caveat does not make the earlier impossibility statement valid.
- A family-specific ownership preference is a possible economic hypothesis,
  but the existing recent-parent ownership gap already depends on space needs,
  ownership preferences, fertility sorting and wealth. Assigning this single row
  to another coefficient does not establish separate identification. We do not
  add that parameter merely to relieve the fit.
- Twelve proposals and arbitrary fit cutoffs cannot reject an architecture or
  attribute its failure uniquely to the deposit/price channel. No such cutoff is
  adopted. Bound hits are likewise not proof of a structural conflict.
- The repeated claim of 40% too few resident children needs a matched empirical
  definition and independent reproduction before changing the departure law.
  No new departure schedule is adopted from that claim.
- The income-grid results also change entrant composition. They motivate a
  numerical/entry check, but are not an isolated grid-convergence result. A
  single bootstrap standard error is not automatically the appropriate
  numerical-error tolerance for every calibration row.
- 'Stationary evaluations' must distinguish a full scored objective from its
  internal normalization solves. The proposed overnight panel uses at most 28
  full objective evaluations, with an enforced maximum of eight stationary
  solves per evaluation (224 total), not 28 raw stationary solves.

The next controlled panel therefore keeps the current economic model and
complete target system, starts at the verified selected fit, and estimates
local moment responses in every existing structural coordinate, with selected
half-step checks. This is a calibration diagnosis under a provisional income
candidate. It cannot settle that candidate's empirical measurement or adopt a
new baseline. Any subsequent changed model requires a coherent measurement
contract and its own joint refit.
