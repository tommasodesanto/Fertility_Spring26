2. **The gradient is unproven as an explanation, not ruled out.** Reweighting fixed policies toward currently low-income states differs from correcting a misspecified child-cost block. Keep the gradient as a substantive validation concern while withholding causal attribution in either direction.

3. **A positive policy contribution is not a welfare statement.** It counts births under changed policies at held exposure. It contains no compensation, resource-cost or welfare calculation. Separately, stationary endpoints can describe household population scale, birth timing/distribution and correctly defined absolute flows under the maintained closure. They do not reveal the transition, and replacement normalization prevents treating the endpoint scale as an unconstrained completed-fertility response. Neither fact establishes welfare gains.

This is a fixed-price diagnostic, not a certified policy equilibrium.

The census makes checking origination timing at least as urgent as changing child preferences. Preserve all targets, impose no desired credit magnitude, and let these validations determine which block—if any—needs revision.
