**Astra max, round 1 — October 4, 2026.** Read-only scientific review of the post-interest, soft-finance chain-13 reference. No solve, specification change or calibration adoption. Scratch magnitudes remain conditional on the recovery team's source checks.

**Judgment.** A weak mortgage-credit fertility response does not by itself invalidate a housing-and-fertility paper. It does invalidate using that response as evidence of a quantitatively important mortgage bottleneck. The present evidence does not establish whether erroneous income sorting or a small child-relative gain from credit is decisive. Those are different hypotheses and can coexist. The current empirical calibration does not separately identify this interaction: housing responses *to* a birth discipline housing demand, not the causal fertility response *to* financing.

**Established in the executed model.** A first-birth attempt succeeds with age-specific probability \(\pi_j\). Let \(V_1\) be the value after a successful first birth, including optimized housing and tenure, and \(V_0\) the value of waiting. With first-birth cost \(f\), the attempt probability is
\[
p_s=\Lambda\!\left[\pi_j(V_{1s}-V_{0s}-f)/\kappa_f\right].
\]
Thus a financing change works through \(\Delta V_1-\Delta V_0\); neither the ownership response nor the total value of credit identifies that difference. Locally, the realized birth-hazard response is \(\pi_j^2p_s(1-p_s)(\Delta V_1-\Delta V_0)/\kappa_f\). Large benefits concentrated on households already almost certain either to wait or to try also produce small responses. “Parents are rich” does not identify these marginal households. See [household.py:970](/Users/tommasodesanto/Desktop/Projects/Fertility/Fertility_Spring26/code/model/production/engine/household.py:970).

The implemented purchase condition is \(Rb+y+S\geq(1-\phi)Q\), where \(y\) includes the current four-year income flow, \(S\) is sale proceeds and \(Q\) the purchase price. Spending satisfies \(b'=Rb+y+S-Q-c-K\); new-owner debt satisfies \(b'\geq-\phi Q\). Existing owners additionally have the grandfathered stayer rule. This is already substantially softer than a liquid-wealth-only down payment. Equal borrowing and saving rates remove a rate wedge, but do **not** remove the shadow value of binding borrowing constraints. See [purchase mapping:896](/Users/tommasodesanto/Desktop/Projects/Fertility/Fertility_Spring26/code/model/production/engine/household.py:896), [transaction kernel:387](/Users/tommasodesanto/Desktop/Projects/Fertility/Fertility_Spring26/code/model/production/engine/kernels.py:387), and [stayer floor:908](/Users/tommasodesanto/Desktop/Projects/Fertility/Fertility_Spring26/code/model/production/engine/kernels.py:908).

Parents face a 2.594-room floor; it does not rise with additional children. Rental housing can reach six rooms. Owner sizes are discrete, and the ownership service premium is modest. These objects make rental substitution a plausible reason for a small child-relative credit benefit, but their presence is not evidence that substitution explains the observed response. The active floor is near its 2.600 upper bound while early fertility remains 0.534 against 0.810. **The theory does not promise a positive aggregate fertility effect.** Its buyer must satisfy \((1-\phi)Ph\leq b\), explicitly before income arrives: [theory section:100](/Users/tommasodesanto/Desktop/Projects/Fertility/Fertility_Spring26/latex/simplified_olg_paper_theory_section.tex:100). Its compensated-reallocation proposition holds fertility, tenure, population and prices fixed, assumes market-rate bridge finance and household-specific two-date transfers, and requires the value gap to exceed real transfer costs: [appendix:421](/Users/tommasodesanto/Desktop/Projects/Fertility/Fertility_Spring26/latex/simplified_olg_paper_theory_appendix.tex:421). This is a conditional implementability result, not a welfare theorem for universal down-payment loans in the quantitative economy. Its separate fertility derivative explicitly balances extra space against the goods cost of buying it and can have either sign: [appendix:513](/Users/tommasodesanto/Desktop/Projects/Fertility/Fertility_Spring26/latex/simplified_olg_paper_theory_appendix.tex:513). Weak quantitative fertility therefore need not contradict the theorem, but the financing mismatch prevents presenting the exercise as a direct test of it.

**Corrections to the proposed diagnosis.** CRRA utility with a fixed additive child benefit can favor richer households: in a homothetic specialization, a proportional child-resource cost has a utility cost proportional to \(y^{1-\sigma}\), which falls with income when \(\sigma>1\). A constant proportional time cost preserves that scaling argument. This is not a theorem about all constrained dynamic states, nor proof that realistic labor-supply or childcare mechanisms cannot matter. The active solver actually rejects its optional earnings-penalty switch with native purchase-income timing: [household.py:707](/Users/tommasodesanto/Desktop/Projects/Fertility/Fertility_Spring26/code/model/production/engine/household.py:707).

The recovered “benefit world” changes a means-tested **cash-resource floor**, introduces unfinanced transfers, and renormalizes \(\psi\); it is not merely an income-dependent child-utility correction. **Three decisive checks, in priority order.**

1. **Complete the common-state decomposition.** Its first stage above already rejects a uniform null. Recover \(\Delta(V_1-V_0)=\kappa_f\Delta\operatorname{logit}(p)/\pi_j\) on unsaturated states; the saved attempt-value gap is \(\pi_j(V_1-V_0-f)\). Separate first/later births, age, income, financial wealth and beginning tenure; trace the negative exposure contribution before calling it debt crowd-out. Small relative gains throughout occupied states support the structural-benefit hypothesis; substantial gains outside the weighted \(p(1-p)\) region support a mismatch between financing gains and birth margins. A sign reversal concentrated in later indebted states would motivate a debt-path check, not establish it. Preserve the successful pre-fertility inversion: saved `g_beginning_distribution` is post-fertility, so cannot be used directly: [distribution.py:704](/Users/tommasodesanto/Desktop/Projects/Fertility/Fertility_Spring26/code/model/production/engine/distribution.py:704).

2. **Count actual financing and family-space exposure.** On those same baseline states, tabulate which owner products become affordable under higher \(\phi\), which would fail the theory's strict cash test, the ending-debt floor, and rental-cap exposure. Compare occupied housing services and tenure changes in both saved arms. If ownership moves while family services and child-relative values barely move, rental substitution is supported. If binding soft constraints and extra family space coincide with the birth margin, the weak response needs another explanation. If only the strict gate bites there, the theorem/model timing difference is the leading issue. This census needs no Bellman solve and does not license adopting stricter finance.

3. **Make one matched income-gradient validation.** Use baseline-era fertility data, comparable age exposure, the same capped-child definition, the same household/woman mapping and comparable income quantiles; state precisely whether income is current household resources or predetermined earning capacity. Cross-tabulate liquid wealth when available. A persistent mismatch in low-wealth fertility and birth timing is a relevant failure of policy incidence; disappearance under harmonization withdraws the original diagnosis. Current income is partly an outcome of parenthood, so this table alone cannot identify causal preferences or credit elasticities.

**Paper decision.** Retain the unchanged model provisionally for a sharp conditional distinction: financing can change tenure substantially while fertility depends on access to additional family space and the child-relative value of resources. A modest average effect can coexist with important incidence and welfare effects, which must be measured rather than presumed. This narrative fails if matched relevant household margins are badly wrong, numerical failures drive the response, or supposedly consequential housing access leaves fertility unchanged throughout empirically relevant states. Stationary population scales are not transition fertility effects or welfare rankings.

Modify only the block rejected by evidence. A financing revision needs observed underwriting/down-payment restrictions; a transfer revision needs measured benefits and funding; a child-space revision needs incremental room-demand evidence; an earnings-cost revision needs parental earnings/childcare evidence and timing. Added free parameters need additional informative moments or explicit external restrictions. A mortgage spread is not warranted merely to amplify fertility. Preserve the remaining economics and the full target contract while testing any one justified change.
