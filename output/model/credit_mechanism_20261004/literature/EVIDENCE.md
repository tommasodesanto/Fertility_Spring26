# Focused literature evidence: credit and fertility

Verified October 4, 2026. Eight retained papers; bounded first pass, not an exhaustive survey or an adopted specification. Source page numbers are printed pages in the linked version. The supplied numerical/CPS claims were not independently reproduced here. Cumming–Dettling was screened but replaced in this eight-paper packet by DUE after its identity was recovered.

## 1. The income-gradient claim needs a conditional statement

Jones, Schoonbroodt, and Tertilt distinguish the wage \(w\), full resources \(W=w+y\), and endogenous earnings \(I=w l_w\). In their static separable, common-curvature CES example with parental time costs only and no nonlabor income, fertility increases with wages when \(\sigma>1\), is constant at logarithmic utility, and decreases when \(\sigma<1\). With logarithmic utility, adding goods costs produces a positive gradient. Genuine nonlabor resources, unequal utility curvature, endogenous human capital/tastes, child quality, leisure, and two-parent opportunity costs can change the result. Their dynastic proportional-scaling result assumes permanent wage differences; it is not a theorem about transient current household income. Thus “time costs cannot fix it” is too broad; “a proportional earnings deduction alone does not reverse the homogeneous CRRA-2 scaling force” is defensible. [JST, pp. 52–56, 91–93](https://www.nber.org/system/files/chapters/c8406/c8406.pdf).

**Own analytical implication, conditional on the supplied utility form:** let \(Q(c,h)\) be a material composite homogeneous of degree one and let a child reduce effective material consumption from \(Q\) to \(Q/e\), with fixed utility reward \(\psi\). For \(U(x)=x^{1-\sigma}/(1-\sigma)\), the material utility difference obeys

\[
U(\lambda Q/e)-U(\lambda Q)
=\lambda^{1-\sigma}[U(Q/e)-U(Q)].
\]

At \(\sigma=2\), doubling all material resources halves this loss while leaving \(\psi\) unchanged. A wage-proportional time cost has the same scaling property in an otherwise homogeneous problem. A housing necessity, borrowing boundary, cash floor, or changing future earnings breaks exact homogeneity. This establishes a force, not the sign of the complete lifecycle solution. The current means-tested cash floor and a hypothetical child-utility normalization are distinct objects; the source pass does not endorse conflating them.

**Measurement implication:** compare births and childlessness at the same age using pre-birth resources, persistent earnings rank, liquid wealth, and female earnings separately. Current household income can reflect childbirth-induced labor supply and schooling/timing. An observed age-25 income gradient cannot by itself identify \(\psi\), child time costs, or a permanent-resource gradient.

## 2. Rate spreads are real precedents; they do not eliminate rental substitution

**KMV (Kaplan, Mitman, and Violante):** \(r_b=3\%\), \(r_m=r_b(1+\iota)\), \(\iota=0.33\): approximately 4% mortgage amortization interest, a 1-percentage-point annual wedge. This is not a 33-point spread. Risk-dependent origination price \(q_j<1\) creates heterogeneous effective borrowing rates beyond the common amortization rate. Mortgages are long-term/defaultable; origination has both LTV and payment-to-income limits. Table 1 permits rental sizes \(\{1.17,1.50,1.92\}\) and owned sizes \(\{1.50,1.92,2.46,3.15,4.03,5.15\}\). Section 5.3 finds credit's house-price effects remain weak under alternative rental segmentation. This is evidence about housing, not fertility. [September 2019 author version, pp. 9–12, 19–20, 43; JPE 2020](https://gregkaplan.me/s/kaplan_mitman_violante_sep2019.pdf).

**SSV (Sommer, Sullivan, and Verbrugge):** deposits earn 4%, collateral debt costs 5.5% (1.5-point additive spread). Borrowers satisfy \(m'\le(1-\theta)qh'\), with \(\theta=.20\). Mortgages behave as interest-only HELOCs, with no compulsory principal repayment or default. Renters can obtain the same shelter grid as owners plus a smaller rental-only unit; utility from shelter is tenure-invariant. Changes in both borrowing and saving rates alter landlord investment and rents. These institutional choices differ from KMV. [JME 2013, pp. 857–858, 860–861, 864](https://www.kamilasommer.net/RentPriceRatio.pdf).

**DUE (*Dynamic Urban Economics*, Greaney, Parkhomenko, and Van Nieuwerburgh):** the retained February 16, 2025 version uses the same externally fixed \(q=.02\) for signed liquid wealth. At a purchase, \(\widetilde b=b+(1-\psi)Ph-Ph'\), with \(\widetilde b\ge-\phi Ph'\). Earnings then arrive continuously through \(\dot b=y+qb-c-\text{rent}-(\delta+\tau_H)Ph\). The origination test therefore uses cash and net sale proceeds; it does not advance several years of subsequent earnings. Equation 2.27 is \(r=(\delta+q+\tau_H)p-\dot p\); omitting capital gains is valid at a stationary price. This is a continuous-time housing precedent, not an empirical validation of either discrete-time purchase rule. [Retained primary PDF, pp. 11–12, 18, 28](/Users/tommasodesanto/Zotero/storage/P6QPKJR8/Dynamic_Urban_Economics.pdf); [NBER record](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33512).

## 3. Credit-access evidence differs from housing-wealth evidence

**Dettling–Kearney:** metro house prices interact with predetermined 1990 ownership shares of demographic groups; the natality records do not observe each mother's tenure. The implied effects of a $10,000 price increase are +5% births at 100% ownership and −2.4% at zero ownership, not directly measured individual tenure treatment effects. Owners' wealth/equity and nonowners' purchase costs move together; the design does not isolate mortgage eligibility. [Revised June 2016 NBER version of JPubE 2014 paper, abstract, pp. 2–3, 10–14](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w17485/w17485.pdf).

**Lovenheim–Mumford:** PSID owner housing-value gains identify a resource margin. Table 6 estimates, per $100,000 four-year value gain, −0.0007 (SE .0025) for initially childless owners and +0.0305 (SE .0048) for owners with one child. The lowest-income quartile is unresponsive. This contradicts treating their average owner-equity response as direct evidence that credit should move poor households' first births. The lifetime calculation is an extrapolation from period responses. [ReStat 2013, pp. 469–474, Tables 3 and 6](https://jenni.uchicago.edu/Fertility/Lovenheim_Mumford_2019_ReStat_v95_n1.pdf).

**Hacamo:** a U.S. federal-regulator ruling supplies mortgage deregulation exposure; this is a closer credit-access design than either price paper. The author reports increased home purchase and childbearing, with supplemental tests favoring space over income/wealth growth. The abstract's 6-point statement covers “purchasing a home and having a child”; without the underlying outcome table it must not become a 6-point births-only calibration target. Full-paper retrieval was blocked and the author-linked appendix is deleted. [Author abstract](https://isaachacamo.github.io/research.html); [published RFS 2021 record](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/34/2/907/5864039).

**van Doornik et al.:** Brazilian consórcio lottery timing instruments credit access while participants continue predetermined payments. Equity extraction is prohibited before repayment. Table II estimates +1.15 probability points for household children and +.016 children; ages 20–25 respond more. The treatment bundles access, dwelling quality, neighborhood and possible rent savings. Importantly, p. 16 defines households by residential addresses; Table A.1 calls the outcome children of the household. Their “completed fertility” label is not automatically maternal children ever born. Verify that measurement before transporting a completed-birth target. [November 10, 2025 working paper, pp. 12–16, 19–20, Tables II–III/A.1](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=152292).

## Decisive implications for the lead

1. **Preserve the model if its claim is conditional:** mortgage relief may shift tenure substantially while changing births little when adequate family space is rentable. A spread is empirically defensible but does not establish a fertility mechanism or desired effect size.
2. **Test birth-margin sorting before changing preferences:** a wrong resource gradient can place prospective parents far from a purchase constraint. Correct observer alignment first; then distinguish an actual transfer schedule from a preference normalization. Neither can be selected because it generates a larger credit response.
3. **If claiming credit causes family formation, identify the missing access margin:** measure pre-birth liquid funds and purchase timing, and rental availability/quality for families by income and location. A multi-year income allowance at origination or an artificial rental cap needs evidence. The direct credit papers motivate these margins but do not identify the model's GE property-tax counterfactual.
