# Saved household policies after a first birth

Run `NUMBA_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 MKL_NUM_THREADS=1 code/model/.venv/bin/python output/model/credit_mechanism_20261004/mechanism_slides/household/build_household_policies.py` from the repository root. The script reads saved arrays and performs **zero model solves**. It writes [the age-22 policy figure](household_policies.pdf), [the all-age matched-state figure](household_by_age.pdf), PNG copies, [plotted policy values](household_policies.csv), and [a source and validation receipt](receipt.json).

## Economic reading

1. The first figure holds beginning wealth, age 22, childlessness, renter status and earnings state fixed; orange is a successful first birth and blue is waiting in the same period.
2. A successful first birth raises expected **physical rooms** throughout the displayed occupied wealth range, from 5.38 versus 3.54 rooms at zero beginning wealth.
3. This extra space need not come through ownership: at zero wealth, the owner-choice probabilities are 0.01% after success and 10.96% while waiting.
4. Expected ending financial wealth responds non-monotonically: at zero beginning wealth it is 0.422 times annual earnings after success versus −0.081 while waiting, but the ordering reverses above roughly two times annual earnings.
5. Across all fertile ages and beginning states, baseline attempt-mass weights give 6.761 rooms after success versus 5.213 while waiting; ownership is 46.28% versus 48.51%.
6. These are choices conditional on a realized birth outcome in a fixed-price saved solution; neither figure isolates a causal effect of the child-space floor or a policy equilibrium.

The age-22 illustration uses the fifth of nine Markov earnings states, $z=0.778$, which is the exposure-weighted median among **all** childless tenures at that age. Its four-year after-tax budget income is 2.061 model units and current annual gross earnings are 0.560 model units. The starting household in the figure is a renter. Both beginning and ending wealth are divided by that same earnings level; raw model-unit values remain in the CSV. The displayed beginning-wealth range is 0 to 4.524 times annual earnings (0 to 2.535 in raw model units), covering 99.668% of positive prebirth exposure for that age, earnings state and starting tenure. All 28 positive-exposure nodes are retained in the CSV, with `displayed=0` beyond the plotted range. The all-age figure uses the already checked [T1 native tabulation](../../birth_tenure_tabulations/README.md): baseline prebirth childless attempt-mass weights, seven four-year age cells, and all beginning wealth, tenure and earnings states. The script reaggregates those age rows to the table's overall row before plotting.

For each starting state and realized birth outcome, the script normalizes the saved tenure-choice probabilities as the native forward pass does. It evaluates conditional consumption, ending assets and renter rooms at both post-transaction wealth interpolation nodes from `build_forward_tenure_transition_maps`. A renter who remains a renter uses the saved stayer controls; purchases use ordinary controls. It then averages over **all** destination tenures. An owner destination contributes its physical `H_own` room quantity; renter rooms contribute only under the renter choice. The success state is $(n,m)=(1,1)$, valid here because the executed case uses constant child maturation; the waiting state is $(0,0)$.

The [receipt](receipt.json) pins the saved arrays, executed inputs, extraction helpers and native source. Prebirth inversion reproduces native first-birth flows to $4.0\times10^{-17}$. Across every positive-probability option at the 28 occupied age-22 nodes, the maximum transaction budget discrepancy is $5.33\times10^{-15}$, the tenure-probability sum discrepancy is $2.22\times10^{-16}$, and excluded positive exposure is zero. The minimum saving-floor slack is $-8.88\times10^{-16}$, numerical roundoff. This validates the plotted policy accounting, not full model convergence or the author's empirical fit.
