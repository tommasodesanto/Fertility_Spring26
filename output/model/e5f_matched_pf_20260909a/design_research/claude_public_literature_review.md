# Public-only independent literature review

This is a research lead sheet from Claude, not verified project conclusions. Only public questions and web access were supplied; no project files or details were sent. Lead-verified sources and corrected interpretations appear in the decision report. Claims marked unverified here are not promoted.

Both subagents completed and I closed two of their open items myself. Findings below.

## Headline findings

**Scope 1.** Neither housing paper infers shocks from the price path. Sommer-Sullivan-Verbrugge impose observed changes in rates, downpayments and income as an unanticipated permanent shock off a pre-boom steady state, then solve a perfect-foresight path. Kaplan-Mitman-Violante embed the whole 2001-2007 episode as a low-probability realization of a known stationary Markov chain under rational expectations, with belief-process probabilities calibrated to OECD boom-bust size and duration rather than to US prices. Both build their initial economy from mixed-vintage moments without an explicit defense of the pooling.

**Scope 2.** Borella-De Nardi-Yang is not joint across cohorts. The NBER text says the two cohorts are estimated "separately," with only the discount factor and consumption weight transplanted to the younger cohort. The closest genuine precedent for one objective combining common preferences with cohort-varying drivers is Eckstein-Keane-Lifshitz (2019), whose supplement confirms a single stacked MSM criterion over all five cohorts. Greenwood-Seshadri-Vandenbroucke (2005) is the cleanest household-macro precedent for estimating structural parameters and a dated technology path jointly by minimum distance along a deterministic perfect-foresight transition.

## Taxonomy and what the literature does and does not establish

**Verified facts.** Four distinct designs exist in print. Deterministic perfect-foresight path fitting with jointly estimated structural parameters and dated drivers exists in Greenwood-Seshadri-Vandenbroucke. Cohort-stacked partial-equilibrium MSM with common preferences and cohort-varying drivers exists in Eckstein-Keane-Lifshitz. Stochastic aggregate likelihood exists in Liu and Plagborg-Møller. Steady-state calibration followed by imposed shocks and a computed path is the housing-literature norm in Sommer-Sullivan-Verbrugge, Favilukis et al. and Greenwald-Guren, while Kaplan-Mitman-Violante replace the MIT shock with a stationary regime-switching process.

**Inference.** I found no published heterogeneous-agent GE household model that estimates common structural parameters and time-varying aggregate drivers in one objective along a historical transition with an explicitly dated initial distribution. That gap is a finding about the public literature, not a claim about any private model.

**When pooled micro restrictions can calibrate an initial state.** The housing papers pool moments implicitly under three assumptions, none of which they state. First, the pooled moments must be invariant to the driver that moves along the transition, so a wage-process persistence or an intertemporal elasticity can come from any vintage only if it is a deep parameter, not an equilibrium outcome. Second, the pre-shock window must be close enough to stationary that averaging across it does not bias the initial distribution, which is the assumption the 1995-2005 homeownership average in Sommer et al. and the ten pooled SCF waves in Favilukis et al. both require. Third, moments from a different population, such as the OECD boom-bust panel in Kaplan et al., must be treated as priors on a process, not as dated observations of the economy being simulated.

**Which dated outcomes must be matched at their dates.** Any moment that is itself an equilibrium object responding to the transition driver: house prices, rents, price-rent ratios, homeownership rates, leverage, foreclosure rates, participation rates, fertility rates, and cross-sectional wealth or debt distributions. Borella-De Nardi-Yang and Eckstein-Keane-Lifshitz handle this correctly by indexing every moment and every policy input by cohort and calendar year, and by truncating age windows to data availability. Sommer et al. and Kaplan et al. match such objects only at the pre-boom date, then compare an untargeted path to the data.
