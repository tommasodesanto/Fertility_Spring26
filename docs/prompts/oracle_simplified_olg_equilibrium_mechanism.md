# Settle the economic mechanism before another theory rewrite

Act as an independent economic theorist and a demanding coauthor. We need one useful analytical result for a short illustrative model accompanying a richer quantitative housing–fertility paper. The exact current theory note and an adjudicated Claude review are attached. Do not rewrite the full note or return a menu of loosely related theorems. Work carefully on the missing economic step, check your own proposed statement against all household constraints and equilibrium conditions, and return a concise recommendation with the complete proof behind it.

The intended mechanism is simple: young families need housing for children, a mortgage requires a down payment, rental homes have limited size, and older households may occupy relatively large homes. An equally weighted planner reallocates housing toward the young. We have a settled allocation problem and several correct conditional results. We have NOT yet obtained an economically persuasive primitive equilibrium proposition in which ownership and the rental limit actually matter. Prior reviews kept mistaking an allocation identity for that missing theorem, or trading one elaborate sufficient condition for another. Resolve this rather than reproducing it.

## The question to solve

Can the EXISTING model deliver, under a simple and economically interpretable region of primitives, (i) a competitive allocation in which young families receive too little housing for the specified planner, with (ii) genuinely different tenure choices and an active rental limit, and preferably (iii) old households occupying at least as much housing on average? Derive the competitive allocation or sufficient bounds from household choices and market clearing. Then apply the existing planner result. Income–wealth heterogeneity must remain. Specify individual gains as well as the aggregate result where possible.

Old homes being larger is the author's desired economic picture, not an assumption already established. If your condition only gives the weaker planner shortfall, state what it predicts about the age profile. Do not silently replace the desired picture or claim it follows merely because the old have accumulated some equity. Conversely, do not treat failure of an unrestricted age ordering as proof that no useful conditional theorem can exist.

## What is already proved and the traps to avoid

For an uncapped fixed-fertility planner, aggregate young housing rises exactly when alpha times mean old housing exceeds gamma times mean young space net of children's needs. With alpha=gamma, the gain per young household is one half of [kappa mean(n) - (mean(h_y)-mean(h_o))]. This is the transparent allocation identity, not the missing primitive equilibrium theorem. Capped planner gains need the precise capacity conditions in the source, or a new valid argument; competitive cap slackness does not automatically imply planner cap slackness.

The explicit income benchmark sets phi=q, has all young financially constrained, and keeps competitive caps and the old financial-estate floor slack. Conditional real choices then coincide across tenure, and rental limits do no work. With E=1+alpha+vartheta, K=1+gamma+omega_B, w=y_y+b, v=y_o, its result follows from v_i/w_i>beta K/(qE) and mean(v)/K>mean(w)/E, plus the exact price/cap conditions. This theorem is valid but a weak leading illustration. A separate appendix constructs an active-cap family using high old income; do not present its existence as if the economic concern were thereby answered.

The Claude review correctly diagnosed the phi=q specialization, then made errors that have been explicitly rejected. For a constrained owner at stationarity:
 a' = -(phi/q) P h_y,
 z = v + (1-phi/q) P h_y,
where v includes the rebate. Thus phi=q removes net house equity at unchanged prices. General owner resources remain a'+P h_y+v; the constrained formula does not cover savers. Restoring phi<q does not itself prove larger old homes: if v=0, the old cash budget c_o+a_e+(1+q tau)P h_o=(1-phi/q)P h_y with c_o>0,a_e>=0 implies h_o<h_y. Positive-tax rebates generally rule out v=0; do not pass that diagnostic off as a positive-tax equilibrium.

On the slack old-estate/housing regime, let x=c_y-chi n and s=h_y-kappa n. The Euler ratio is c_o/x=(beta/q)(1+mu/Lambda). A positive young financing multiplier can overcome beta<q. Beta>=q is SUFFICIENT in one route, not NECESSARY. The attached assessment supplies an exact heterogeneous equilibrium with beta R_f=1/2, old homes three times young homes, and both planner gains. This counterfamily refutes an impossibility claim; its high old income is not plausibility evidence.

A young housing cap alone does not make c_o>x at beta=q if finance has zero multiplier. An active inequality may have a zero multiplier. Positive aggregate strictness needs positive mass. At phi=q, financing has no extra housing-versus-current-consumption wedge, but can STILL create a total housing allocation gap via inter-age resources. Do not conflate those two claims. Closed forms derived with slack young caps must not be used for capped renters. Eliminating multipliers is not equivalent to eliminating endogenous prices, rebates or tenure composition.
