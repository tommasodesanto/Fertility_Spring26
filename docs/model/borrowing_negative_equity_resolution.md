# Borrowing and negative-equity resolution design

**Status:** read-only specification recommendation, September 26, 2026. This is not an author-adopted model change. No model code, frozen run, calibration, or manuscript was changed. The lead should verify the equations against the active solver before implementation.

## Recommendation

Near term, retain the current signed-asset rule for the already frozen September 25/26 comparisons and the next matched baseline, while explicitly adopting and labeling it as a reduced-form borrowing assumption. This is executable today: no new unsecured lending (`lambda_d = 0`), an age-dependent rollover rule for inherited unsecured balances, a current house-value borrowing floor for owners, and sale proceeds net of the sale wedge. Do not call it a standard amortizing mortgage or “no refinancing.” The code does not track mortgage principal separately, and its default owner rule permits additional secured borrowing up to the current collateral floor even for a stayer.

There is no evidence in the reviewed project record that identifies a replacement repayment schedule. Therefore do not substitute an arbitrary constant-rollover rule or a guessed 30-year mortgage. Record the 42-to-62 taper as an inherited, economically consequential maintained assumption and test it before interpreting debt-sensitive results. If the paper instead claims a conventional amortizing mortgage, that is a new specification requiring a separate mortgage-balance state, an externally specified contract schedule, and explicit creditor/default/terminal accounting. The optional current switches are diagnostics; they do not create that full contract.

## What the active code does

**Important scope limitation in the current-income adapter:** it passes \(s=0,D=0\) to the owner-saving kernel (adapter lines 263–265), whose native floor uses \(s,D\) to roll over owner debt in excess of collateral value (kernel lines 1238–1244). That makes the owner saving floor the plain current collateral floor in every owner state. It prevents a purchase-stage shortfall from rolling into a new mortgage, as intended, but it also suppresses rollover for an incumbent owner who is underwater after a price fall. The frozen stationary comparison does not establish behavior in those underwater states. Treat the adapter as unsupported for price-transition/underwater-owner use until the floor is transaction-specific: preserve the declared incumbent-stayer debt rule while enforcing the purchase rule on origination, then test both branches. This is a source-level discrepancy for any transition or inherited-underwater use, not evidence that the frozen stationary results are invalid.

Let (b) be signed liquid financial wealth, with owner mortgage borrowing represented inside negative (b), (P h) the house value, φ the maximum financed share, (u=b+φ P h) the owner's unsecured excess relative to the collateral floor, (s_{j+1}) the next-age rollover weight, and (D_{j+1}) the unsecured credit-line cap. The default owner next-period floor is

\[
b' \ge -\phi P h + \min\{s_{j+1}\min(u,0),-D_{j+1}\}.
\]

For a renter all debt is unsecured:

\[
b' \ge \min\{s_{j+1}\min(b,0),-D_{j+1}\}.
\]

These are source equations, not a separately adopted economic contract. `lambda_d=0` sets (D=0), so it disallows new unsecured borrowing but does **not** erase an inherited unsecured balance. The default age weights are 1 through age 42, linear from 1 to 0 over ages 42–62, and 0 thereafter. At age 62, renters must have nonnegative next-period wealth; owners may still carry debt up to the secured collateral floor. Source: [parameters.py](/Users/tommasodesanto/Desktop/Projects/Fertility/Fertility_Spring26/code/model/intergen_eqscale_seq_optimized/parameters.py:718) and [solver.py](/Users/tommasodesanto/Desktop/Projects/Fertility/Fertility_Spring26/code/model/intergen_eqscale_seq_optimized/solver.py:141).

On a new purchase, the cash test is ((1-\phi)P h), the collateral floor is (-\phi P h), and the transaction wealth before current income is (x=b+\text{net sale proceeds}-P h). The owner budget then includes income once. The current-income adapter relaxes the purchase-stage threshold by (y/R), leaves (x) unchanged, and retains the final mortgage floor; this avoids counting (y) both in purchase wealth and again in the period budget. The author-agreed direction is that current income can finance a purchase. This was implemented in an isolated adapter and retained in the frozen September 25 pair. The Sept. 26 four-utility run uses the remote frozen contract named in `CALIBRATION_STATUS.md`; neither frozen run is to be edited. See [e5f_earnings_wealth_contract.py](/Users/tommasodesanto/Desktop/Projects/Fertility/Fertility_Spring26/code/model/tools/e5f_earnings_wealth_contract.py:243), the [Sept. 25 run record](/Users/tommasodesanto/Desktop/Projects/Fertility/Fertility_Spring26/output/model/native_financing_diagnostic_20260919/specification_followup/target_review_v1/payroll_tax_review/overnight_pair_20260925_preparation/README.md), and the frozen Sept. 26 contract pointer in [CALIBRATION_STATUS.md](/Users/tommasodesanto/Desktop/Projects/Fertility/Fertility_Spring26/CALIBRATION_STATUS.md:8).

For a renter buying, the forward transaction map sets post-purchase wealth to the larger of (b-P h) and (-\phi P h), subject to grid support. An owner selling becomes a renter with (b+(1-\psi)P h); a replacement purchase uses sale proceeds less the new purchase price. Thus a sale shortfall follows the household as renter debt rather than disappearing in the housing map. See [solver.py](/Users/tommasodesanto/Desktop/Projects/Fertility/Fertility_Spring26/code/model/intergen_eqscale_seq_optimized/solver.py:4085) and the sale value construction at [solver.py](/Users/tommasodesanto/Desktop/Projects/Fertility/Fertility_Spring26/code/model/intergen_eqscale_seq_optimized/solver.py:2580).

## Hand checks

Use (P=1), (h=4), φ (=0.80), ψ (=0.06), and λ_d (=0). A buyer needs cash of (0.8), and the secured debt floor is (-3.2). At exact eligibility, (b=0.8) implies (x=b-4=-3.2), exactly the collateral floor.

If an owner owes (b=-3.2) and the house price falls to (P=0.75), the new secured floor is (-2.4), so (u=-0.8). At an age with (s=1), the owner floor remains (-2.4 + (-0.8)=-3.2): the price decline does not trigger a margin call. At age 52, (s=0.5), so the floor is (-2.4-0.4=-2.8); the rule forces 0.4 of debt paydown over the period. This age-dependent tightening is a model assumption, not implied by the LTV limit.

After selling at (P=0.75), net proceeds are ((1-0.06)(0.75)(4)=2.82), leaving renter wealth (-3.2+2.82=-0.38). With (s=1), that shortfall rolls over; with (s=0.5), the next-period floor is (-0.19), requiring 0.19 repayment. This verifies the intended transfer of a sale shortfall into renter debt. It does not establish who absorbs unpaid debt at death or terminal liquidation.

Current-income threshold check: with (b=0.50), (y/R=0.30), the same buyer has resources for the required (0.80) down payment. The adapter lowers only the temporary purchase gate by 0.30. The transaction remains (x=0.50-4=-3.50), and the budget has (R x+y=R(-3.20)); income is counted once. A gate that shifts the down payment but not the purchase-stage collateral test would reject this case; adding (y/R) to (x) as well would double count it.

## Economic rationale and model alternatives

The distinction between new origination and the existing balance is economically important: applying a new LTV cap to all incumbent debt can force deleveraging after a price decline even when the loan contract has not changed. Kaplan, Mitman, and Violante model long-maturity mortgages, origination LTV restrictions, lender pricing, default, refinancing, and HELOC access, so their paper supports distinguishing origination from incumbent debt; it is not a direct justification for this project's age taper ([published paper PDF](https://violante.economics.princeton.edu/sites/g/files/toruqf5621/files/documents/kaplan-et-al-2020-the-housing-boom-and-bust-model-meets-evidence.pdf)). Hurst and Stafford document a consumption-smoothing/refinancing channel through home equity, which reinforces that permitting or prohibiting equity extraction is a substantive choice ([author paper](https://erikhurst.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/hurst_stafford_jmbc_final.pdf)). A full contract with default and priced mortgages is a richer alternative, not a required feature of every housing-fertility model; Campbell and Cocco is one example ([Journal of Finance article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.12252)).

Three usable specifications should not be conflated:

1. **Current mixed debt rule:** one net signed wealth state; LTV floor plus age-tapered rollover; no unsecured credit line. Cheapest and already frozen, but the age schedule and cash-out interpretation require explicit labeling.
2. **Reduced-form nonrecourse mortgage with rollover:** preserve pre-existing debt through price declines; constrain new borrowing at origination; prohibit cash-out by incumbent owners if that is the intended no-refinancing institution; use a stated amortization/rollover schedule; assign residual shortfall to borrower, lender/default process, or estate under one explicit terminal rule. With only one net-wealth state, principal and liquid assets cannot be separately audited.
3. **Full mortgage contract:** add mortgage balance, maturity/interest/amortization, refi/default choices, and lender/counterparty accounting. This is a substantial state-space and transition change; it is not a parameter-only patch.

The recommended near-term route is specification 1 with those limitations explicit. Specification 2 requires an author-adopted schedule and creditor/estate rule. If no such evidence is available by Sunday, the scientifically defensible resolution is an explicit decision to retain the reduced-form rule for the current baseline while leaving its institutional interpretation and terminal settlement as named limitations, rather than inventing a mortgage contract.
