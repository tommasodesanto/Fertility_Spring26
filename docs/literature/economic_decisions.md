# Economic decision library

Living reference for decisions that otherwise require reopening the same literature and implementation notes. It links source conventions to the project's experimental choices; a literature precedent is not an author decision. “Open” means the author has not adopted the object as a production specification. Page references below are printed pages.

| Object | Literature convention and source | Project direction or experiment | Author status | Next decisive check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Earnings components | Sommer (2016, JME, pp. 29–32) uses an age profile plus persistent AR(1) and iid transitory shocks, without a fixed permanent type ([paper](https://www.kamilasommer.net/Fertility.pdf)). Meghir–Pistaferri (2004, pp. 5, 11–13) show richer permanent/transitory/measurement-error dynamics and identification limits ([paper](https://web.stanford.edu/~pista/meghir.pdf)). | Persistent plus iid is the intended architecture; no fixed type. Direct four-year PSID gross-household earnings experiment estimated period ρ=.7761, persistent innovation SD=.4374, iid SD=.1650; iid precision is weak. | Intended architecture; estimates experimental, not adopted. | Check own-data component identification and residual measurement error; verify the reported infeasible-value contamination before more calibration. |
| Frequency and income measurement | De Nardi (2004, ReStud, pp. 765–766) estimates on complete five-year PSID income cells ([paper](https://users.nber.org/~denardim/research/denardi.pdf)); Bick (2016, JEEA, Appendix C.1) constructs three-year gross-income flows from monthly averages before estimating ([paper](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/41757/1/MPRA_paper_41757.pdf)). Neither supplies a generic four-year conversion. | Model period is four years. Existing PSID construction is household gross labor earnings; the direct-period estimate is an experiment. Annual Sommer values are not four-year inputs by simple ρ^4 plus averaged iid. | Income concept retained; four-year process construction remains open. | Reconcile annual/biennial observation schedule and source sample, then validate a direct-cell or simulated-aggregation constructor against covariance, tails, and household choices. |
| Persistence evidence | Sommer's ρ=.95 is annual (2016, Table 2, p. 31); De Nardi's reported five-year estimates vary by specification (2004, Table A.1, p. 765); Bick's .882 is a three-year autoregressive coefficient (2016, Table C.1, p. 46). | Direct four-year estimate above is one source-specific candidate. Cross-paper coefficients are not interchangeable across frequency, sample, or income definition. | No imported persistence adopted. | Compare matched-sample longer-lag covariances and age variances; check the available earlier PSID years and block alignments before refitting. |
| Initial wealth at entry | De Nardi (2004, p. 764) sets entry assets to zero in a model that also includes later inheritances; Sommer (2016, p. 30) starts at age 18 with zero holdings and persistent state zero. Chambers, Garriga, and Schlagenhauf (2009, p. 702) match PSID ages 20–23 nonhousing wealth/earnings by income state and warn that zero wealth biases against early homeownership ([PDF](https://cooperative-individualism.org/chambers-matthew_accounting-for-changes-in-the-homeownership-rate-2009-aug.pdf)). These are context-specific alternatives. | Project direction is heterogeneous empirically disciplined entrant wealth. An overnight zero-wealth-at-age-18 case was only an externally fixed diagnostic fallback after inherited-debt cases failed feasibility. Lead recommendation, not adoption: use age-appropriate empirical entry wealth conditional on income as primary; keep zero-wealth as sensitivity and resolve debt separately rather than silently truncating it. | Heterogeneous wealth intended; empirical joint entry law and mapping open. Zero wealth is not adopted. | Resolve source age/sample discrepancy (18–24 versus 25–35), wealth concept, income-rank coupling, and entry age; report distribution and debt feasibility. |
| Initial persistent earnings dispersion | Bick (2016, p. 21) draws initial shocks from the stationary distribution of its estimated process; Sommer (2016, p. 30) instead sets the initial persistent shock to zero. | Project direct four-year estimate initializes persistent risk at age 18 using a candidate stationary distribution; no fixed type. | Approximation under experiment. | Compare stationary initialization with the empirical entry distribution and inspect wealth-income rank dependence; do not conflate dispersion with a fixed type. |
| Initial iid earnings shock | Sommer (2016, pp. 30–32) includes iid transitory risk; Bick's reported process is a single AR(1), not a separate iid component. | Independent iid draw at entry is part of the experimental persistent-plus-iid process. The current age-18 initialization convention has not been independently validated. | Open. | Confirm whether entry observes an iid realization or begins before the first period shock; check resulting first-period income and entrant wealth dependence. |
| Purchase-income timing | Sommer, Sullivan, and Verbrugge (2013, pp. 857, 859–860) let households observe the current labor-income shock before beginning-of-period housing choices ([paper](https://www.kamilasommer.net/RentPriceRatio.pdf)); Chambers et al. (2009, pp. 689, 693) charge current income and purchase costs in the same-period budget. | Isolated adapter lets current income enter purchase eligibility once, while preserving transaction wealth and mortgage limits. A realized four-year total known before purchase would disclose within-period future earnings and is a stronger information assumption. | Direction agreed for experiment; production adoption awaits full validation. | Verify timing against the chosen four-year income object and test budget/transaction accounting and household equilibrium. |
| Consumption and transfer floors | The reviewed income papers do not establish this project's required consumption or transfer rule. | Current experiments set transfer-floor parameters to zero and retain a parenthood housing requirement. Claude reports that the stored consumption minimum is not enforced as an economic floor; operative treatment must be checked. | Model convention, rationale open. | Document economic rationale and verify low-income feasibility and boundary allocations under the selected earnings risk; do not cure failures by silently shrinking risk. |
| Rental access and housing floor | Sommer et al. (2013, pp. 857–860) model young households as renters; that precedent does not establish a rental-access or child-space restriction for this project. | Current candidate keeps renter access and a positive housing-services floor when children are at home; exact parent/child state mapping must follow the active code. | Implementation retained provisionally; economic justification and author adoption open. | Check renter/owner feasibility and rooms by dependent children, plus the housing-floor evidence in the ledger; distinguish children at home from children ever born. |
| Preferences and bequests | Sommer (2016) is a fertility-risk architecture reference; De Nardi (2004) models bequests in a family OLG setting. Their preference units and transfer institutions are not portable ([Sommer](https://www.kamilasommer.net/Fertility.pdf), [De Nardi](https://users.nber.org/~denardim/research/denardi.pdf)). | Existing preference and estate equations are retained in diagnostics; fitted coefficients are model-specific. Estate utility, actual estate receipts, and entrant wealth are separate objects. | No new preference or transfer adoption. | Inspect implied fertility, saving, bequest probabilities/flows and receiver rules across wealth and child states; assess weak local bequest identification before interpreting coefficients. |
| Fertility and population normalization | Sommer (2016, Table 2, pp. 31–32) calibrates to its own fertility targets; the reviewed earnings papers do not settle this project's conversion between model households, births, and resident persons. | Current calibrated normalization targets completed fertility 2.1; the active decision ledger leaves literal-person versus household/population units unresolved. | Numerical normalization retained; interpretation open. | Reconcile model fertility, child survival/residence, entrant household flow, and total-population labels before making paper-facing population claims. |

## Scope and provenance

This is a navigation and decision aid, not a new literature review or model specification. The earnings rows summarize the bounded reviews in [`annual_sources_review.md`](../../output/model/native_financing_diagnostic_20260919/specification_followup/earnings_wealth_v1/literature/annual_sources_review.md), [`multiyear_review.md`](../../output/model/native_financing_diagnostic_20260919/specification_followup/earnings_wealth_v1/literature/multiyear_review.md), and [`timing_review.md`](../../output/model/native_financing_diagnostic_20260919/specification_followup/earnings_wealth_v1/literature/timing_review.md); parameter interpretation is governed by [`parameter_validation_rubric.md`](../../output/model/native_financing_diagnostic_20260919/specification_followup/earnings_wealth_v1/literature/parameter_validation_rubric.md) and current author choices by [`ACTIVE_DECISION_LEDGER.md`](../model/ACTIVE_DECISION_LEDGER.md). No claim is made that unreviewed objects (notably floors, rental access, preferences, or population units) have a complete literature basis. Keep those rows explicitly open until a source or author decision closes them.
