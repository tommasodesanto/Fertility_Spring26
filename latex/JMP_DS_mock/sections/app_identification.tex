\section{Identification}
\label{app:identification}

The ten calibrated parameters $\theta$ of Section~\ref{sec:quant-identification}
minimize
\begin{equation}
\sum_{k=1}^{10} w_k\bigl[m_k(\theta)-\hat m_k\bigr]^2,
\label{eq:q-loss}
\end{equation}
where $\hat m_k$ is a data moment, $m_k(\theta)$ its counterpart in the
stationary equilibrium at parameters $\theta$, and $w_k$ the inverse square
of a tolerance for the moment. At each $\theta$, the house price makes
completed fertility equal to replacement, and $H_0$ is the supply scale at
which that price clears the housing market with one unit of households.

Table~\ref{tab:app-loss} reports, for each target, the tolerance that sets
its weight in the loss \eqref{eq:q-loss} and its contribution to the loss at
the estimates. Table~\ref{tab:app-jacobian} reports the elasticity of each
targeted moment to each estimated parameter, from a 1\% change in the
parameter, solving the equilibrium each time. The flattest direction of the
loss raises $h_P$ and $\kappa_T$ together: along it, the loss rises by one
when $h_P$ and $\kappa_T$ move by about 6\%, while no other parameter moves
by more than 2\%.

\paragraph{The response of births to housing costs.}
The policy experiments change the cost of housing and ask how births
respond. In the model this response runs through three objects, and the
moments of Section~\ref{sec:quant-identification} discipline each of them.
The first is how much space a child requires: a household with children at
home pays for $h_P$ rooms every period, whether it rents or owns, and the
recent-parent ownership gap and mean rooms pin $h_P$. The second is what
this space costs: mean rooms and ownership select the 2007 price at which
births are at replacement. The third is how strongly the decision to try
for a child responds to its value. From \eqref{eq:m-fertility},
\begin{equation*}
 \frac{\partial\pi^F_{t,a}}{\partial\bigl(V^F_{t,A}-V^F_{t,0}\bigr)}
 =\frac{\pi^F_{t,a}\bigl(1-\pi^F_{t,a}\bigr)}{\kappa},
\end{equation*}
so the scales $\kappa_1$ and $\kappa_C$, disciplined by the timing of births
and their spread across mothers, govern how far a given change in the cost of
a child moves births.
