\vspace{2pt}

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item Credit does not raise completed fertility. It brings some births forward: $+0.4$ to $+0.6\%$ in the first period, fewer afterwards.
\item The negative effect in the second row needs many childless owners in two-room homes: 30\% in the model, 3\% in the PSID.
\item With the US size distribution the effect is zero.
\item Credit for parents only flips the sign, to $+1.2\%$, still small: parents can already rent family-sized homes.
\item In the data the null is timing plus higher prices (boom buyers paid 2.24 times income for the same space, against 1.77); the model's extra channel, the starter-home trap, is not in the data.
\end{itemize}
\vspace{2pt}
Credit changes when households buy and when they have children, not how many.
