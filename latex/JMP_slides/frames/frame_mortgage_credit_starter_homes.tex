\titlelink{loan-not-insurance}{Back}
\footnotesize
Loan-to-value 80\% $\to$ 95\%, fixed prices. Families can rent up to six rooms.
\vspace{2pt}

{\centering
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.05}\setlength{\tabcolsep}{7pt}

Homes for sale & Ownership 18--29 & Births, first period & Completed fertility \\
\midrule
Without 2- and 4-room homes & 36\% $\to$ 40\% & $+0.1\%$ & $-0.05\%$ \\
With 2- and 4-room homes & 44\% $\to$ 69\% & $+0.4\%$ & $-1.4\%$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\par}
\vspace{2pt}

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
\item Credit does not raise completed fertility. It brings some births forward, $+0.4$ to $+0.6\%$ in the first period.
\item The negative effect needs many childless owners in two-room homes. The model has 30\%, the PSID 3\%.
\item With the US size distribution the effect is zero.
\item Credit for parents only turns it positive, $+1.2\%$, still small.
\item {\color{draftred}In the data the null is timing and higher prices. Boom buyers paid 2.24 times income for the same space, against 1.77 before. The model's starter-home trap is not in the data.}
\end{itemize}
\vspace{2pt}
Credit changes when households buy and when they have children, not how many.
\end{frame}
