\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{-1pt}
\item No quantum effect. The LTV constraint does not enter the user cost of space; the marginal household rents at $b=0$. Branch values rise by $+0.008$ (child) and $+0.007$ (no child).
\item Tempo effect only. Liquidity brings forward births of new buyers and leveraged owners in the first period; along the GE path births are $-0.3$ to $-0.8\%$ thereafter.
\item Model mechanism: a starter detour. One fifth of childless renters become owners of two-room units below the parent floor of 2.6; childlessness at 46--49 rises from 19.0\% to 20.2\%, progression to a second child is unchanged. Without the two- and four-room rungs completed fertility is unchanged.
\item Data (PSID). Childless owners aged 22--37 in four rooms or fewer: 23\% against 68\% in the model; in two rooms, 3\% against 28--31\%. Buyers of 2003--07 bought the same space as 1997--2001 buyers at 2.24 times income against 1.77; small owners upsize and have second births at the same rates as others.
\item Credit does not raise completed fertility. The model's small negative effect comes from a mass of two-room owners that the PSID does not show; with the US size distribution, credit changes tenure and price, not births.
\end{itemize}
