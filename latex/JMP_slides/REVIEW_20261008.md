1. **Equilibrium, Population.** "$N_{0,t+1}=B_t$" says newborns are adult households next period, but the engine uses an entry queue. Proposed: "Births $B_t$ form households after the childhood lag, at the entry ages; adults survive with $s_a$." Confirm the queue timing first.
2. **Financing display.** Given the budget and $b_{t+1}\ge-\phi P_th_t$, the inequality is redundant. Proposed: "Down payment: debt after a purchase is at most $\phi P_th_t$."
3. **User-cost identity** $r_t=(R_b+\delta_H+\tau^p_t)P_t-P_{t+1}$. It charges maintenance and tax at $t+1$, while the budget charges them at $t$. Check the timing.
4. **Selling cost.** $\psi^s$ clashes with $\psi_t$ on Preferences.
5. **Depreciation.** "Depreciates at rate $\delta_H$" → "owners pay maintenance $\delta_HP_th_t$".
6. **Intro and This Paper.** The policy and wealth bullets predate the credit-versus-risk results.
7. **Conclusion bullet 4.** It claims a fertility response to the tax. Live numbers: births −2.9% at fixed prices; in the long run, price −5.3% and population +0.7%.
8. **Childlessness ages.** The labels mix 45, 46–49 and 40–44.
9. **$\alpha_0$ source.** CE 2019–23 does not match the CES rent-share target 0.360.
